Part 1 · LinkedIn analysis

Reach: one paid post accounts for nearly all of it

194,643 impressions in 365 days sounds big, but 174,724 (89.8%) were sponsored, and most came from one boosted Phocuswright post in November. Organic reach was 19,919 impressions for the whole year.

Organic vs sponsored impressions

Admin analytics highlights. The 30-day window (Oct 20 – Nov 18) holds 165,470 impressions, of which 162,894 (98.4%) are sponsored.

Engagement totals

Reactions, comments and reposts for the full 365 days vs the last 30 days. The 30-day figures grew a lot compared with the prior period (reactions +400%, reposts +400%, comments flat).
The sponsored post in context. "Fort Lauderdale, here we come…" (Phocuswright, Nov 11) received 183,619 impressions, 52 clicks (0.03% CTR), 2 reactions and 3 reposts. Its engagement rate was 0.03%. In the same period, the organic Snowbound Expo post got 194 impressions with a 2.06% CTR and a 7.73% engagement rate, as reported by LinkedIn. The 183,619 figure comes from the per-post table (Nov 6–20) and covers two more days than the 365-day total, so it can exceed the 174,724 sponsored figure.

Followers: an engineering audience in LATAM, not a buyer audience

The page gained 929 followers in 365 days (928 organic, 1 sponsored), with spikes around Jan 18 (≈55/day) and in August (≈75/day). The last 30 days added 86. Most followers are Zaelot's own talent market: engineers, Entry and Senior level, in Uruguay and Argentina. Director-and-up roles (Director, VP, CXO, Owner, Partner) total about 382, or 6.9%.

What people respond to

These patterns are based on 33 posts. The sample is small, so treat them as directions to test, not rules.

1

Partnership and milestone news gets the most engagement

Hightouch Certified Composable Partner (56 reactions · 10 reposts), Esri official partner (27 · 4 reposts), and the Claude skill for Hightouch launched at Databricks Summit (23 · 8 reposts). These average 35.5 engagements per post, more than twice any other topic. People share news that is a credential for them too.

2

Being at the event beats announcing you'll be there, especially with photos

The best organic post of the year is the Digital Travel Connect wrap (San Diego, sponsored event): 2,221 impressions, 49 reactions, 4 comments, 10 reposts. NSAA at Snowbird (1,255 impressions, 18 reactions) is next. Image posts get a 3.04% organic engagement rate, compared with 0.76% for link posts.

3

Co-hosting with partners multiplies engagement

The Hightouch Ad Studio hackathon in NYC got engagement from three directions: Jaki's post (36 reactions), Jeff's repost of Hightouch (31), and the "Calling all NYC marketing leaders" repost (14). That makes it the most-engaged event theme of the year.

4

Link posts and commentary reach people but don't get reactions

The 11 link posts have the highest median organic impressions (≈577) but average only 5 reactions and a 0.76% engagement rate. Commentary on outside news (Braze acquisition, Ask Jeeves, Hightouch hire) averages 4 engagements, so the commentary needs a stronger Zaelot point of view.

5

Specific stories about Zaelot reach further

"We had a problem. Agentforce had a solution." (Zaelot's own timesheet case) is the #2 organic post by impressions (1,341), and the human-written New Year post reached 990. Both are first-person stories. Client case studies and results are almost entirely missing from the feed.

6

Video and pure CTAs underperform so far

The 4 video posts average 3.8 engagements and a 1.36% rate. The "Business Agent on OpenAI — HMU" CTA got 6 impressions. These were mostly event livestreams and podcast links, so native short clips (what Part 2 proposes) haven't really been tested yet.

Posting cadence: about three posts a month, with gaps

The page published 33 posts in about 11.5 months. There were none in July 2026, one post each in January, March, August and September, and a burst of 7 in October around the event season. Jaki Levy authored 26 of the 33 posts (79%).

Honest observations

What the numbers say about where the page is today.

Reach Reach is paid-heavy

89.8% of the year's impressions were sponsored, and one boosted post produced 183,619 impressions but only 2 reactions. Organic reach averages about 1,660 impressions a month.

Engagement Organic engagement is low in absolute terms

The page got 330 reactions and 12 comments in a year, with a median of 5 reactions per post. The 1.80% organic rate isn't bad, but the absolute numbers are too small to build pipeline.

Audience The audience is talent, not buyers

Engineering and IT make up 44.0% of followers and Entry level makes up 41.7%. US metros in the top 10 total 4.9%. Visitors skew a little more toward BD and Sales, so buyers do look. They just don't follow.

Cadence There's too little volume to learn from

At about 3 posts a month, nearly all from one author, the page can't test formats or build a habit with followers. Going to 3 a day is a 30× change, so it needs a production system, not more effort from the same person.

Strength There's plenty of credible material

Hightouch, Esri, Snowflake, Vercel and Salesforce partnerships, plus travel and ski industry events, are real proof. The posts that use this material perform best.

Gap Several story types are missing

There are no client case studies, no engineering how-tos, and no posts on the AI/MCP sales-stack story. Those are Zaelot's most differentiated stories, and none appear in the feed.

Content pillars

Six pillars, ranked by what performs and what's missing

Pillars 1–2 are proven on the page today. Pillars 3–4 are the biggest gaps for Zaelot's positioning in AI and engineering services. Every post should fit a pillar.

Pillar 1 · Proven

Partners & milestones

Certifications, partnerships (Hightouch, Esri, Snowflake, Vercel, Salesforce), launches, and co-built tools.

Evidence: 35.5 avg engagements per post, the highest of any topic.
Pillar 2 · Proven

Field & events

Photos from the event, what we heard there, co-hosted hackathons and dinners, with travel, hospitality and ski as the home turf.

Evidence: the top organic post (Digital Travel Connect, 2,221 impressions) and the hackathon cluster.
Pillar 3 · Gap

AI-native engineering

How Zaelot engineers ship with AI: Claude skills, MCP, agents and evals. Include real code and real numbers, plus how-to posts and short demos.

Evidence: the Claude-for-Hightouch skill post got 23 reactions and 8 reposts. It's core to the positioning.
Pillar 4 · Gap

Client results & case studies

Problem, approach and measurable outcome, with client approval. Include short versions, carousel versions and long blog versions.

Pillar 5 · Reframe

The sales stack / MCP story

How Zaelot runs sales on Claude Code with MCP, told by Jeff. It shows the work we can build for clients.

Evidence: new topic, so it's untested. Measure it against Pillar 1.
Pillar 6 · Support

Industry POV & people

Commentary on travel tech, martech and AI news, always with a clear Zaelot opinion. Also team, culture and remote-first life in Montevideo, Buenos Aires, Reykjavik and Valencia.

Evidence: commentary averages 4 engagements today, so it needs a stronger angle. Culture fits Instagram and also helps recruit the engineers who already follow us.

Video plan

Video: short clips every week, longer pieces when there's a story

Today's 4 video posts were livestreams and podcast links, and they averaged 3.8 engagements each. The plan below tests native, captioned, vertical-first video instead.

Short-form 3 / week

  • Format: 30–75 sec, vertical 9:16 plus square 1:1 cut, burned-in captions, and a hook in the first 2 seconds.
  • Where: LinkedIn native, X, and Instagram Reels (same clip, different caption).
  • What: engineer demos ("watch Claude + MCP do X"), one insight from an event, a client result in 60 sec, and Jeff on the sales stack.
  • KPIs proposed: 3-sec view rate ≥ 30%, average watch ≥ 40%, and engagement rate above the 1.80% page baseline.

Long-form 1–2 / month

  • Event recaps (3–5 min): shot at each conference using a checklist (booth, talks, 3 attendee soundbites). Publish within 72 hours.
  • Client stories (4–8 min): problem → build → result, with an approved client voice. These are also sales enablement assets in HubSpot.
  • Build sessions (15–30 min): the weekly source recording. Post it in full on YouTube or LinkedIn and cut it into clips.
  • KPIs proposed: views per piece, clips produced per piece (≥ 4), and use in sales follow-ups (tracked in HubSpot).
Proposed target: raise video from 12% of posts today (4 of 33) to 30% of LinkedIn posts by day 90. Keep the format only if video beats the image-post baseline (3.04% organic engagement rate) by day 90.
Separate initiative

Email newsletters & event-support emails

This runs separately from social posting, with its own owner, run in HubSpot. It covers a newsletter for owned-audience nurture, plus pre-, during- and post-event sequences tied to a shared conference calendar that lists each event's needs.

Newsletter monthly → twice a month

  • Audience: clients, prospects and partners in HubSpot (opt-in). This is separate from the Substack readership, which is public.
  • Contents: 1 client result, 1 AI-engineering insight, upcoming events ("meet us at…"), and partner news. Most of it is reused from the month's content.
  • Cadence: monthly for the first 90 days, then twice a month if engagement holds.
  • KPIs proposed: open rate and click rate (baseline set on issue #1), replies and meetings booked, and unsubscribes < 0.5% per send.

Event emails per conference

  • Lists: target accounts and contacts attending the event, existing clients nearby, and partners co-exhibiting.
  • Goal: booked meetings before the event, conversations at the event, and opportunities after it.
  • Owner: the event lead named in the calendar below. Sales sends 1:1 follow-ups and marketing sends sequences.
  • KPIs proposed: meetings booked per event, post-event reply rate, and opportunities created within 30 days.

Event email sequence (template)

Before T-21 → T-1

  1. T-21 · "We'll be there": why we're going, who's attending, and a link to book a meeting.
  2. T-10 · Value email: a relevant case study or POV tied to the event theme.
  3. T-3 · Logistics: booth, talk or dinner details, a calendar hold, and a "reply to grab 15 min" ask.
  4. T-1 · Final reminder: to booked meetings only.

During Day 1 → last day

  1. Morning-of: "Here's where to find us today" to booked meetings and VIPs.
  2. Same-day: 1:1 notes after each conversation, logged in HubSpot.
  3. Social sync: on-site photos and clips go out on LinkedIn, X and Instagram (Pillar 2).

After T+1 → T+21

  1. T+1 · Thank-you: a personal note with what we discussed and a next step.
  2. T+7 · Recap: event recap video or blog, plus a related case study.
  3. T+21 · Nurture: a newsletter invite and a soft meeting ask for anyone without a next step.

Rollout

30 / 60 / 90 days

Ramp up to 3 a day instead of starting there. Each phase has a check before moving to the next.

Days 1–30 · Foundation
  • Approve goals, pillars and owners. Set up tracking (UTMs, HubSpot content source).
  • Open or claim X, Substack and Instagram handles. Record baselines.
  • Build the weekly source-recording habit and the Claude/MCP repurposing workflow.
  • Cadence: 1 post/day (LinkedIn weekdays + X).
  • Send Phocuswright post-event emails. Fill in the conference calendar.
Check: 4 weeks of source recordings banked, baselines recorded, and newsletter issue #1 sent.
Days 31–60 · Build
  • Launch the Blog cadence (1/wk) and Substack (weekly).
  • Start short-form video: 3 clips a week.
  • Publish the first 2 client case studies (with approval).
  • Cadence: 2 posts/day.
  • Run the first full pre-event sequence for the next confirmed event.
Check: organic engagement rate holds at ≥ 1.8% as volume grows, and the backlog covers 2 weeks.
Days 61–90 · Scale
  • Add Instagram (4/wk) and the first long-form video.
  • Employee amplification program (Jeff, Jaki, engineers).
  • Cadence: 3 posts/day across channels.
  • Review results against the day-90 targets. Cut what isn't working and double down on what is.
Check: 6,000 organic LinkedIn impressions per month and 150 new followers per month (proposed).

Method & data notes

Data gaps and caveats

  • Post dates are approximate, read from "By [poster] [date]" labels as of Nov 20, 2026, and cover about Dec 1, 2025 – Nov 18, 2026. One post (Esri) had no date and is placed by its "4w" age.
  • Row #23 was dropped as a duplicate of #22 (Hightouch Certified Composable Partner). A sponsored ad placeholder was excluded. 33 posts were analyzed.
  • Clicks and LinkedIn's engagement rate weren't captured for the 365-day range, and the per-post table loaded only for Nov 6–20. Engagement rates here are computed as (reactions + comments + reposts) ÷ organic impressions and are lower than LinkedIn's click-inclusive figure.
  • Summed per-post reactions (367) exceed LinkedIn's 365-day total (330) because reposts of partner and employee posts show the original post's reactions.
  • Two posts (#17 Claude skill, #22 Hightouch partner) had no impression count and are excluded from impression and rate calculations.
  • Visitor and demographic data covers the last 30 days (visitors) or all current followers (demographics). It isn't a time series.
  • There's no baseline yet for Blog, X, Substack, Instagram or email. Targets for those channels are set after the first 30 days.

A short read

What changed over time

This is a reading of the sourced points above, not a complete company history. Headcount figures disagree across sources, and website copy often lagged events by months.

2020–2022 · A family firm that scaled fast

Public sources agree Zaelot was incorporated in 2020 by the Lombard and Clark brothers, starting from a group of four. By April 2021 the company was hiring at scale (14 open roles). By late 2022 the website said 70 people in 9 countries, and a new board member described “80+ team members” in the Americas and Europe. We have no LinkedIn follower count from this period.

2023 · Two deals, then a new parent

Axon Consulting joined in early 2023 (Goode and InnovationMap; one article says a 100-person firm in 14 countries). In July, Gangverk acquired Zaelot and described more than 90 professionals in 15 countries, with a combined team just over 200. After that, Zaelot kept its own brand and LinkedIn page. The company website still said “70 people in 9 countries” as late as April 2023, so the site is a lagging source.

2024–2025 · The page grows, the archive does not

The only public snapshot of the LinkedIn page, from Feb 7, 2024, shows 2,138 followers and 70 listed employees. By the time admin data begins on Nov 19, 2025, implied followers are about 4,645 — the faster stretch of the growth we can measure. Mid-2024 website copy said 90 professionals in 11 countries and stayed on that line into 2026. Of official company posts before the admin list, we only found three, all from Dreamforce in October 2025, already in the Agentforce / Salesforce voice that defines the last 12 months.

The last 12 months · The baseline in this report

From Dec 1, 2025 through Nov 18, 2026 the page posted about three times a month, gained 929 followers, and now sits at 5,574. Organic reach is modest; one sponsored Phocuswright post accounts for most impressions. The live website now says 120 professionals in 11 countries. The content approach in Part 2 is a plan from this baseline, not from a recovered older posting cadence — that cadence was never recovered.

What is still missing

  • Any LinkedIn follower count before Feb 7, 2024, and any snapshot between that date and Nov 19, 2025.
  • The company-page post archive for 2020 through September 2025. Search found no official Zaelot posts from those years; Wayback did not store the /posts feed.
  • Engagement, impressions, clicks and video views for every pre-admin post, including the three October 2025 Dreamforce posts.
  • A single, consistent headcount series. Sources disagree (70, 80+, 90, 100, 120) and website copy lagged.
  • A page-creation date. LinkedIn About shows the founding year, not the day the company page was opened.
