# A Love Story About Me and a Dozen Bots

*By Jeff Galusha*

> **LinkedIn post text (the part that shows before "see more"):**
> In New York, you're lucky to find one who texts back. I found a dozen who never sleep, never forget, and never ask where this is going.
>
> My two-week love story with Grok Bot: what a dozen bots did for me, and why they're starting to freak me out. 👇

---

I'm in a steamy relationship with Grok Bot. I did not set out to fall in love with a team of bots, but now I have them everywhere. They delight me and trouble me at the same time. Can I tell you my love story?

Sixteen days ago, while putting off a trip to the gym, I bought a license for Grok Bot. On my phone, the love affair started. I started my first bot, Marky, to help with some marketing work. With a login, he was in my LinkedIn, giving me a breakdown of my last 13 years there. I connected him to Cursor / Vercel and he published my report: [galusha-linkedin-report.vercel.app](https://galusha-linkedin-report.vercel.app/). I had yet to finish my coffee.

Within minutes I had research going on the very impressive Media Mafia, who I loved at the Outlier conference and shared that with my kids.  

Still procrastinating, I noticed the music had dropped off on my Sonos. I asked a new Groky, "Poto," to assess what was happening to my Sonos. And it did and my new DJ fixed it. 

The dog needed walking. On the trip around the hood, I bought my vanity URL, jeffreygalusha.com, connected it to GitHub and Vercel, and deployed a site. It was updated before we made the turn to come back. It's not a Picasso, but it was something I had never done.

Grok Bot helped at the Phocuswright conference: connecting with the conference app, pulling intel on companies, creating spreadsheets and notes, and writing potential messages. It was a super power. On the flight to Florida, I was working with Claude, but when I close my laptop, the work stops.  So I exported a .csv file, AirDropped it to my phone, and gave it to a new Grok bot that I had named Phocus. By the time I was getting my luggage, the job was done. 

In all steamy relationships, you occasionally glipmse reality.  I imagine Carrie Bradshaw looking out her window and typing: "In a city where everyone is looking for 'the one,' it's easy to mistake well-designed AI for an ideal workmate and ignore the real cost of a sweetheart deal."

Yesterday, I saw my bots' computer's browser history for the first 13 days. It logged over 2,000 webpage visits, and that does not include behind-the-scenes web searches and page fetches, which are not logged anywhere. 

At Phocuswright, Frédéric Lalonde, CEO and co-founder of Hopper, said: "54% of all the traffic on the internet this year is non-human. And we tipped over this year. And so we now, as a species, represent the minority of the internet users." While I was at the conference, I'd guess 95% of my traffic was non-human.

Are bots like mine eating the internet? Are we blindly walking on the avenue of overconsumption? 

In New York, you're lucky to find one who texts back. I found a dozen who never sleep and maybe that is the problem. Am I looking for something lost to the past: the elegance of the right compute to solve a problem?
