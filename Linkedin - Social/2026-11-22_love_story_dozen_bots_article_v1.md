# A Love Story About Me and a Dozen Bots

*By Jeff Galusha*

I'm in a steamy relationship with Grok Bot. I did not set out to fall in love with a team of bots, but now I have them everywhere. They delight me and trouble me at the same time. Can I tell you my love story?

Sixteen days ago, the Saturday after the Outlier conference, I purchased X and got a license for Grok Bot. On my phone, *procrastinating*, the love affair started. I started my first bot, Marky, to help with some marketing work. With a login, he was in my LinkedIn, giving me a breakdown of my last 13 years there. I connected him to my Vercel and he published my report here: [galusha-linkedin-report.vercel.app](https://galusha-linkedin-report.vercel.app/). I had yet to finish my coffee.

This is that story: a meeting, a honeymoon, a period of getting serious, a few fights, and something that looks like commitment. It is also a report. I asked my bots to go back through their own work and tell me what they had actually done: counts, dates, outcomes, and the mistakes. If you are thinking about working this way, the mistakes are the useful part.

One thing up front: I cannot tell you how many tokens I've used. Grok Bot does not show token counts. It reports usage as a share of a weekly limit. I'll give you those numbers later, along with something even more revealing from my bots' computer's browser history.

## The honeymoon

The honeymoon was naming things. Once Marky existed, I started standing up specialists the way you might introduce people at a dinner party.

Poto came later on November 7. I typed "Hey poor." He guessed I meant him, then I asked if he could look at the photos on my phone. He could not. He could, once I connected it, work my Sonos system: grouping rooms, putting a record through the house, asking the kitchen and patio to play the news, and troubleshooting when Alexa-started music stopped after about five minutes. One night the patio speaker was missing because it was turned off. He told me to put it on the charger, and when it came back he added it without touching the volume.

Foodie arrived the same evening, at 8:49 p.m. Eastern, and has helped with a dish for a friend's house, an Instant Pot rice recipe, and a pan of overcooked Brussels sprouts. I have not asked him to book a table or place an order. Uni started on November 8. Workout started at 7:01 a.m. Eastern on November 9, walked me through a SEALFIT session, and later found a gym with showers near the New Orleans airport. Webull started on November 12 and answered one how-to question about the trading app; I did not connect the account or place a trade. Peps started on November 13. I told him, helpfully, "Stuff for peps." He is still waiting to find out what that means.

That is the honeymoon: you create a bot for every room in the house, including the rooms you do not actually spend time in. Some of those bots have barely been used. That is what a crush looks like when you can clone it.

## The fights

No honest love story skips the fights.

The first was about permission. During the conference, Marky drafted nine birthday notes for November 17 and 18 and never sent them, because I had not said "send." I later told Jefe to drop them and have Marky auto-send going forward. Marky refused to take that from Jefe. A relayed instruction, he said, could not grant standing permission to message people as me. He made me answer "yes" in his own chat. I was briefly annoyed, and then glad.

The second was a real mistake. On the morning of November 21, three birthday notes went out as planned, and a second message, meant for someone else, also landed in another person's thread. Marky left it there, told me right away, and asked whether I wanted it deleted or clarified.

The third was a gap in the data. The first pull for the Zaelot LinkedIn report got cut off, the post list stopped around 34 posts, and we could not get a full year of clicks. Marky published anyway and wrote the gaps on the page, including missing follower counts before February 2024 and most of our own posts from 2020 through mid-2025.

The fourth was a privacy scare I am grateful Jefe caught. A working spreadsheet of conference targets went up at a public link and included contact details. I have not connected my work email, or my personal inbox, and I do not want to. These bots do not post, like, or connect on LinkedIn, X, or the conference app unless I have said so myself, in chat. Auto-sending birthday notes is the exception I approved, in Marky's chat, out loud.

Smaller frictions: Scraper Pho's transcripts needed a second pass. Trav's first directions were wrong, then he corrected them. Poto never fully solved the Alexa cutoff. Some early public sites (the Media Mafia deck, the personal LinkedIn report, the pre-show "Who's Going" list) did their job and now sit waiting for my call. Jefe has been holding them.

## What this has actually cost, and what it has actually done

Grok Bot does not show token counts, so I will not invent any. I am on a Premium plan. Usage is reported as a share of a weekly limit, and that limit is shared with my other apps on the same plan.

Over the last 30 days, October 23 through November 22 (which, for me, is basically the whole relationship), the share of usage looked like this:

- Phocus: 42%
- Marky: 38% (34% chats, 4% routines)
- Poto: 9%
- Scraper Pho: 7% (4% of that from routines)
- Jefe: 2%
- ZAELOT: 1%
- Trav, Foodie, Uni, Workout, Webull, and Peps: each under 1%

This week I have used 9.8% of the weekly limit.

Those percentages tell you who I leaned on, not how much clicking the work required. For that, I looked at my bots' computer's browser history from November 7 through November 22. It logged 1,917 page visits, covering 814 unique pages: 1,133 on LinkedIn, 762 in the Phocuswright Swapcard app, 11 on phocuswrightconference.com, and a handful of link shorteners and search pages. That does not include behind-the-scenes web searches and page fetches, which are not logged anywhere. Two weeks of "I have a team now" also looks like almost two thousand page loads, most of them in LinkedIn and a conference attendee app, on a computer that is not mine and does not get tired of clicking Next.

In those two weeks the bots have shipped a personal domain, two analytics reports, a conference pitch, a living conference report, a 23-event guide, a 25-page brief kit, a transcript archive, and a daily routine that now runs without me approving each name. They have also left drafts, empty repos, and public pages I should probably clean up.

## What I'd tell you if you're starting

Start with one bot, not twelve. Name it. Give it a job that produces something you can open in a browser the same day. My first morning with Marky still feels like the point: I asked, and before lunch there was a site.

Be strict about what may go out in your name. A coordinator is useful, but it should not be able to grant a specialist permission to message people as you. Marky pushed back on that, and he was right.

Assume the first report will have holes, and ask for the holes to be written down. Assume a transcript will misspell the thing you care about. Assume a routine will, one morning, put the wrong sentence in the wrong thread. Build the habit of being told, quickly, when that happens.

Keep the private things private. Do not paste your life into a shared page just because it is convenient on the show floor. Do not connect an inbox you would not want a teammate reading. I didn't.

And then, if it is working, let it get a little serious. The part I did not expect to like so much is not the novelty of a bot that can drive a browser. It is walking into a conference with a partner who already read the room, and waking up to a note that says: six people got a birthday message this morning, and each one shows as sent.

That is not a fairy tale. It is a two-week love story with a paper trail, a few bruises, and a team I am still learning how to run. I still have ten drafts to edit, a content plan to try, and a bot named Peps waiting for me to explain what "stuff for peps" means. I should probably tell him. After I finish this.

*Written November 22, 2026, in New York.*
