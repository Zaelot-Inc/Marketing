## The pre-event work, which was substantial

This event had the most rigorous preparation of anything on the 2026 calendar, and it is worth documenting because it explains the capture rate.

**A scored ICP model was built before the show.** The `Top 25 ICP` tab ranks 25 target contacts on a composite score out of 11, combining company size, contact role, budget signal, buying-stage readiness, and stated investment. Rank 1 is Sound Physicians at 10.0, and Michael Parris did become one of the three genuine SQLs. Nine of the fourteen meetings landed came off that top-25 list.

Taiwan Cement sits at rank 25, the bottom of that list, scored 7.5. The model ranked it last and the meeting still got booked and later logged as a deal. That is the ICP model being built and then partly ignored, which is worth noting since the same model ranked WWEX at number 2 and that account was written off on a hunch.

**All 101 attendees were scored against partner fit.** The `Hightouch Agentic Fit` tab reads every attendee's stated project objectives and challenges against three Hightouch products, and it is honest about its own limits: the note says an AI conference crowd skews to agentic infrastructure rather than customer-data activation, and it lists the accounts it excluded as false-positive keyword matches (Cargill, Optum, WSFS Bank, Redwood).

That tab surfaced five Hightouch co-sell targets: **Rocket Mortgage (Dian Xu, Tier 1), Charter Communications (Vinod Sosale Murthy, Tier 1), Kimberly-Clark (Amish Chadha, Tier 2), PepsiCo (Sanjay Ramchandani, Tier 2), and Estée Lauder (Heather Roetman, Tier 3).**

Per the 2026-08-12 follow-up priority list, all five are still sitting in the untouched Tier 3 GDS cohort with no first touch sent, four months on. Estée Lauder is the largest of them at a $1M to $5M band.

Note also that the Hightouch tab targets **Dian Xu** at Rocket Mortgage, while the meeting that actually landed was with **James Carson** at the same company. Two doors into one account, and neither has a brief.

## What this says about event strategy

This event executed most of the playbook and the numbers show it. A scored ICP model built before the show, 101 attendees read and ranked, fourteen 1:1s booked against that list, contacts captured within two days, and deals created within two weeks. Fifteen contacts from 101 attendees including one floor pickup, three genuine SQLs, and $140,000 of real pipeline created.

The preparation is the transferable lesson. Nine of fourteen meetings came off a target list that was scored before anyone got on a plane. That is the difference between attending a conference and working one.

Where it fell short is conversion, qualification discipline, and the tail. Three of four deals lost, the one survivor has a close date seven weeks stale, and the five partner co-sell targets the pre-event analysis specifically identified have never been contacted.

The Taiwan Cement case is the sharper lesson. The ICP model ranked it 25th of 25, the pre-event note questioned whether the meeting was worth taking, and it still became a booked 1:1, a HubSpot `opportunity`, and a $40,000 deal record that sat open for two months before closing lost. Meanwhile WWEX was ranked 2nd and written off with "maybe not a fit." The model was built carefully and then overridden in both directions. A scoring model you do not trust is worse than no model, because it creates the appearance of qualification without the substance.

On cost, **$43,158 for three genuine SQLs is $14,386 each**, against $140,000 of real pipeline created and nothing closed. The $38,000 fee bought fourteen well-qualified meetings, which is the fee working as intended, but the conversion has not followed.

The comparison that matters is internal: the same $38,000 fee at Memphis produced five SQLs and a closed customer. Same vendor, same price, and Memphis converted while Dallas has $60,000 sitting open past its close date. That is the number to take into the 2027 renewal conversation.
