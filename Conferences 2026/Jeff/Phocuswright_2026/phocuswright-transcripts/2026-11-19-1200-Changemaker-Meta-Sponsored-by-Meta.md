## Summary

The session opened with an introduction from Meta's head of industry for travel and auto, framing travel as a $10 trillion market heading toward $15 trillion by 2030, but one still held back by legacy tech silos and fragmented booking channels, call centers, and static apps. The core argument was that Meta is positioning itself to lead a shift from these static channels to AI-enabled conversations, and that most travel brands already invest in chatbots, but legacy bots break down when handling real traveler complexities such as multi-leg changes, payment issues, and disruption triage.

A key statistic cited was that 84% of travelers who have used generative AI for travel-related tasks said it materially improved their experience, which was presented as the opportunity for brands to build a trusted, brand-governed agent. This was tied to a broader $5 trillion opportunity emerging from agentic commerce, as AI collapses the traditional travel funnel. The vision outlined was one where every business can cost-effectively offer customers a 24/7 digital concierge via an AI agent, while customers interface with a single AI agent for all their needs, moving toward autonomous agents managing bookings and service on customers' behalf across platforms where customers already spend their time.

Two specific Meta products were introduced to support this vision: Meta Business Agent, described as an always-on, customer-facing agent in a brand's own voice that turns trip inspiration into a repeatable booking flywheel; and Muse, a consumer-facing conversational agent that helps people find products and experiences across multiple brands. The expectation is that business agents and consumer agents like Muse will increasingly interact seamlessly, turning AI agents from simple deflection bots into true resolution agents.

An illustrative customer journey using a fictional travel provider, Zoomture, and a traveler named Hazel was used to show how these pieces connect: an Instagram ad sparks interest, Muse checks fit against schedule and budget, Zoomture's Business Agent provides pricing and availability, booking can happen directly or via Muse, confirmation and later itinerary changes are handled through WhatsApp, and a later holiday offer sent in the same WhatsApp thread restarts the cycle. The takeaways drawn from this example were that Zoomture stays present throughout the entire path, the relationship lives in one persistent conversation rather than fragmented touchpoints, and each step draws on a different part of Meta's portfolio (ads, utility messages, business agent, marketing messages), with a brand's own Business Agent able to offer personalization no third-party agent can replicate.

WhatsApp was positioned as the critical enabler of this persistent, low-friction relationship, cited as the most opened app on the planet, opened about 23 times a day on average, with 100 million Americans now using it monthly and that number still growing. A product demo video was shown to illustrate the experience in action. The session closed with three takeaways: every conversation is an opportunity to maximize customer lifetime value; AI will only work for enterprises if it drives measurable business outcomes without compromising customer experience, requiring trustworthy quality, safety by design, and consistent brand voice; and WhatsApp, combined with Meta Ads, Muse, and Meta Business Agent, offers a new path to drive impact across the full customer lifecycle while keeping brands in control end-to-end, before handing off to Robert Cole of Phocuswright.

## Key Takeaways

### 📱 WhatsApp is positioned as a scaled relationship channel

Meta argues that WhatsApp’s high engagement and conversational context make it a strong place for travel brands to build persistent one-to-one relationships, especially when combined with ads, utility messages and business agents.

Quotes:

> WhatsApp is one of the strongest places to build those one-on-one relationships at scale.

### 🤖 Traditional chatbots struggle with real travel complexity

Existing bots often fail when travelers need help with multi-leg itinerary changes, payment issues, questions or disruption triage—the situations where resolution matters most.

Quotes:

> Legacy bots break on real traveler complexities... Whether it's managing multi-leg changes, payment issues, questions, and disruption triage.

### 🎯 Enterprise AI must prove business value without compromising experience

For AI to work in large travel enterprises, it must produce measurable business outcomes while maintaining trusted quality, safety by design and a consistent brand voice.

Quotes:

> AI is only going to work for enterprises when it drives measurable business outcomes without compromising the customer experience.

### 🔄 Business and consumer agents may transact with each other

Meta distinguishes between brand-owned business agents and consumer agents such as Mews, envisioning a future where they interact seamlessly to find and purchase travel products and experiences.

Quotes:

> We envision a world in the not-so-distant future where business agents and consumer agents like Mews will be able to seamlessly interact.

### ✅ The goal is resolution, not deflection

The emerging standard for travel AI is expected to move beyond routing customers away from human support toward autonomous agents that can resolve needs on the traveler’s behalf.

Quotes:

> AI agents at that point, they go from being deflection bots to being resolution agents.

### 🧑‍💼 Business agents could provide 24/7 digital concierges

Meta envisions AI enabling every business to offer each customer a cost-effective, always-available digital concierge that supports engagement throughout the customer lifecycle.

Quotes:

> At Meta, we see a world where AI enables every business to cost-effectively give each of their customers a 24/7 digital concierge via an AI agent.

### 🧠 Brand-owned agents can deliver deeper personalization

Meta says a business agent can draw on a travel brand’s own data and expertise to provide personalization that a third-party consumer agent cannot replicate.

Quotes:

> A brand's Meta Business Agent can actually draw on its own data and expertise to offer a level of personalization that no third-party agent can replicate.

### ✨ Generative AI is already improving traveler experiences

The speaker cited research showing that 84% of travelers who have used generative AI for travel tasks say it materially improved their experience, raising expectations for brand messaging channels.

Quotes:

> 84% of travelers who have used generative AI for travel-related tasks say that it has materially improved their experience.

### 🔗 Persistent conversations can power the full travel lifecycle

Meta’s illustrative customer journey shows how one ongoing conversation can connect trip inspiration, pricing, booking, confirmations, changes, service and future marketing—reducing friction while keeping the brand relationship intact.

Quotes:

> The relationship lives in one persistent conversation. This is where we think the real value is derived.

### 🛡️ Brands can use governed agents to preserve trust and identity

Travel companies have an opportunity to build customer-facing agents they govern, ensuring conversations reflect the brand while strengthening traveler relationships.

Quotes:

> This is your opportunity to build a trusted agent that you govern. So every conversation reflects your brand.
