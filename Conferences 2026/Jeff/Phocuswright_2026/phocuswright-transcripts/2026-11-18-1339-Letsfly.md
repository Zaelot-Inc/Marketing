## Summary

The session opened with a pitch from Fareportal, the company behind CheapOair and OneTravel, introducing Fareportal Travel OS, a white-label operating system for brands to sell and distribute travel. The pitch framed every purchase as tied to an emotion and a destination, noting that most brands lack their own travel portal even though a handful of banks like Chase already generate billions in travel bookings, leaving a large underserved opportunity for other brands to capture loyalty and revenue by offering branded travel booking directly to their customers.

The platform was described as a multi-sided system with a Partner OS for launching branded travel businesses via point-and-click configuration, a Traveler OS delivering a branded customer experience including automated rebooking when flights are cancelled, and a Distribution OS letting suppliers push targeted offers (such as discounts or bonus points) directly through partner brands. A live demo showed a customer site, goislandbound.com, being configured and launched, as well as a bank-branded travel portal being stood up through a short AI-driven setup process, allowing customers to redeem points for trips. The commercial model was presented as bespoke rather than boxed, with revenue share and commissions tailored to each partner's goals, paired with a 24/7 branded concierge service built on Fareportal's 25 years of servicing experience.

Audience and panel reaction was largely positive, though questions pressed on differentiation and go-to-market focus. In response to questions about funding needs and initial priorities, the first focus was identified as the Partner OS enabling partners to go live quickly. Asked what makes the offering truly innovative versus a souped-up affiliate program, the answer emphasized that unlike affiliate links sending customers off-site, this keeps customers inside the brand's own branded ecosystem, building loyalty and capturing margin on high-value, emotionally charged purchases. On differentiation from other companies building similar solutions, the response again stressed the bespoke, partner-specific approach combined with end-to-end servicing at scale, backed by a dedicated customer success manager, with one large new customer confirmed to be launching by the end of November.

The second pitch came from Letsfly, presenting Postra, a post-booking automation solution aimed at OTAs. The pitch argued that while AI is automating more travel operations, a confirmed booking is not the end of the customer journey, since travelers still need help with changes, cancellations, refunds and ancillaries, and that delivering this servicing well is hard because airlines have different products, rules and fragmented booking channels requiring expert judgment. Postra combines automation with an expert team that prioritizes urgent cases (such as imminent departures), feeding resolved-case data back into the AI engine to continuously improve automation coverage. It supports over 100 airlines across Asia, Europe and the Americas, connects through GDS, NDC, airline direct-connect APIs and aggregation channels, offers a white-label option for travel sellers, and combines servicing with ancillary sales such as seats, bags and meals. A pilot client, a European OTA, reportedly saw operating costs cut by around 70% and servicing success rates rise from 60% to 99.5%, alongside new ancillary revenue.

Audience reaction was broadly positive, but the panel was more skeptical, questioning whether this problem would ultimately be solved at the infrastructure layer rather than through a standalone servicing product, and probing the business model given Postra's relationship to Letsfly itself. In clarifying the model, it was explained that the company operates as a B2B service for client agencies such as OTAs, who pass along booking information like PNR or ticketing details so Postra can handle post-booking servicing on their behalf. When pressed on who the ideal customer would be, given that large OTAs often build this capability in-house, the response maintained that the solution can technically support all types of agencies, including the large OTAs that generate the highest volume of post-booking requests.

## Key Takeaways

### ❓ Letsfly’s customer focus and differentiation remain under scrutiny

Panelists questioned whether large OTAs would build Posture in-house, whether the problem will be solved at the infrastructure layer and what clearly differentiates the product. Letsfly described OTAs and agencies as its customers and said it has one live partner in Europe, but did not identify a more specific ideal customer segment.

Quotes:

> “The large OTAs are doing this themselves… Who would your ideal customers be?”

### 🧳 Fareportal offers bespoke, white-label travel operations

Fareportal positions Travel OS as a supplier-agnostic way for brands to sell travel under their own identity while relying on Fareportal’s platform, operations, servicing and branded concierge. Its commercial model is tailored to each partner’s goals rather than standardized.

Quotes:

> “It’s your brand, our platform. It’s your customers, our operations… It’s bespoke, it’s not boxed, it’s supplier agnostic.”

### 🔄 Travel OS connects partners, travelers and suppliers

Fair Portal describes Travel OS as a multi-sided platform with three connected components: Partner OS for launching businesses, Traveler OS for the branded customer experience and Distribution OS for supplier access to brands.

Quotes:

> “It’s made up of a partner OS for launch. It’s traveler OS for your customers. Getting that branded experience. And it’s distribution OS for suppliers.”

### 🛎️ Traveler OS combines disruption support with branded concierge service

When a flight is canceled, Traveler OS can present pre-prepared replacement options at no additional cost, allowing customers to rebook with one tap. A branded 24/7 human and AI-supported concierge provides additional servicing under the partner’s name.

Quotes:

> “The flight gets canceled, and that’s where traveler OS gives customers flight options at no additional cost, pre-prepared for them… And we have that 24/7 concierge available for them.”

### 📣 Suppliers can target offers through partner channels

Distribution OS allows airlines, hotels and other suppliers to promote directly through selected brand channels, such as a lifestyle brand or bank portal, using discounts, bonus points and other incentives.

Quotes:

> “An airline, or hotel, can now promote directly to specific channels… Direct offers… whether that’s 20% discount on beach getaways or 20% more bonus points on their bank portal redemption.”

### ✅ Posture takes post-booking requests through to execution

Posture is designed to handle changes, cancellations, refunds and ancillary requests—from retrieving available options and rules through confirmation and execution. It supports both servicing and sales of seats, bags, meals, check-in and other extras.

Quotes:

> “Posture is a completion-oriented post-booking automation solution that covers both post-booking servicing and ancillary service.”

### 🧑‍💻 Posture combines automation with expert escalation

Requests that automation cannot complete are routed to an expert team, with urgency-based prioritization—for example, giving precedence to travelers departing soon. Resolution and cost data are then fed back into the automation engine to improve future handling.

Quotes:

> “Not every request can be handled entirely through automation. So what happens when the automation cannot complete a request on its own? We have expert team sitting behind to handle such requests.”

### 📈 Post-booking support can cut costs and improve resolution

In a European OTA pilot, Letsfly says Posture reduced operating expenses by around 70% and increased servicing success from 60% to 99.5%. The platform is positioned to reduce support costs while protecting loyalty and creating revenue from existing bookings.

Quotes:

> “OPEX was reduced by around 70% with Posture… an increase in servicing success rate from 60% to 99.5%.”

### 🖱️ Tenant Hub lets partners configure a travel business without building everything from scratch

Tenant Hub is presented as the partner-facing environment where brands can use point-and-click tools to configure branding, features, customer experience and operational capabilities for a dedicated travel business.

Quotes:

> “With point-and-click, run and launch a completely dedicated branded travel business.”

### 🌐 Posture supports fragmented airline connectivity

Let’s Fly says Posture supports more than 100 airlines across Asia, Europe and the Americas, and can service bookings across GDS, NDC, direct airline APIs and aggregation channels.

Quotes:

> “Posture currently supports more than 100 airlines across Asia, Europe, and Americas… across almost all the channels, such as GDS, NDC, airline direct connect API, and aggregation channels.”
