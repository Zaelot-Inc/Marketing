## Summary

The discussion opened with concrete brand examples of an AI agent (Muse) already in production use. Air France was cited as deploying it across the full customer lifecycle—boarding passes, flight updates, marketing, one-time password authentication, check-in reminders and loyalty—with marketing specifically driving 4.5x better results than business-as-usual email. Uber was highlighted for using the agent to re-engage drivers and riders, seeing a 27% improvement in rider re-engagement and a 16% improvement in driver re-engagement compared with SMS, illustrating that each brand tailors the tool to its own pain points.

On the consumer side, adoption numbers were shared: 10 million people in the US and Canada were using Muse within weeks of launch, a faster ramp than competitors achieved, with recent expansion into Japan, Mexico, Singapore and Australia. Personal use cases described included destination search, hotel recommendations, and proactive flight-disruption alerts tied to weather. The value was framed as compounding over time through two mechanisms: deep connections to email, loyalty accounts and credit cards that let the agent learn personal preferences (car type, family size, preferred airline and hotel), and persistent memory that lets it proactively surface things users struggle to track themselves, such as children's school communications.

On commercial strategy, the current focus was described as improving user experience and expanding geographically before monetization, guided by a stated principle that the agent should act as a fiduciary to the user rather than to brands. A subscription model with a generous free tier and two paid tiers was launched, but described as still early and being refined. Future commerce and advertising opportunities were acknowledged as under discussion at the parent company level, with travel and retail flagged as two of the leading consumer journeys where incremental value for brands is expected to be proven out over time.

The second part of the session turned to tension between airlines and AI booking agents, prompted by a reference to United's reported resistance. Panelists speculated that airlines' core concerns center on verifying the identity of the actual buyer behind an agent transaction, ensuring technical accuracy of agent responses, and avoiding scenarios where a hallucinating agent books something that doesn't exist—raising the question of who is accountable, the airline or the agent provider, when that happens. It was noted that this is still very early, only weeks into these agents' existence, and that control over the booking experience and shelf visibility remains the central issue for carriers.

Despite the United example, panelists stressed that most airlines are proactively reaching out wanting their content represented well in these new channels, viewing agent-driven discovery as an important emerging sales and advertising channel; one approach described using an MCP layer as a 'front door' to validate and control the relevance of incoming agent traffic. Closing predictions on the share of airline tickets agents will book in two years ranged from roughly 15-20% up to 50%, with one view holding steady due to continued direct sales and another predicting the direct/indirect distinction will blur entirely. On who stands to lose, the consensus was that it will be whoever fails to adapt business models and infrastructure to the new agent-driven volume, rather than any single category of player.

## Key Takeaways

### 🗺️ Consumers are using Muse for destination discovery and travel updates

Consumer use cases include searching for destinations, evaluating hotels and finding activities, as well as receiving weather-related flight updates and understanding their potential impact on a journey.

Quotes:

> I'm using it for destination search when I'm planning on traveling somewhere, understanding what hotels to look at, or understanding what to do in those places. And then I think flight updates.

### ✈️ Air France applies agents across the customer lifecycle

Air France uses its business agent for boarding passes, flight updates, marketing, one-time-password authentication, real-time updates, check-in reminders and loyalty. For marketing specifically, it reports a 4.5x result versus its business-as-usual benchmark, identified as email.

Quotes:

> Air France uses it really across the entire customer lifecycle. So we're talking like boarding passes, flight updates, marketing, one-time password authentication, real-time updates, check-in reminders, loyalty. ... They're seeing, for marketing specifically, that it's driving 4.5x results versus our BAU, which I believe is email in that case.

### 🧾 Travel AI complexity intensifies after booking

Unlike many retail journeys, travel’s hardest agentic tasks often come after the sale: cancellations, rebookings, payments and disruptions must be handled reliably so travelers can complete their journeys.

Quotes:

> The complexity in travel actually quite often starts when you finish the search. ... What we need to solve for ... is everything that happens after the booking. The rebookings, the cancellations, the payments part of it. God forbid disruption.

### 📊 AI agents could reshape airline distribution

Panelists estimated that AI agents could account for roughly 15% to 50% of airline bookings within two years. The shift may blur the line between direct and indirect distribution, while businesses that fail to adapt or provide value risk losing share.

Quotes:

> I'll say maybe 50% of bookings will come through an AI channel in some way through agents. ... Steve, final word? 15 to 20%, maybe. ... Whoever doesn't provide value. I think infrastructure providers will need to be there. ... The map is being redrawn, that's for sure.

### 💳 Muse is prioritizing user value before refining monetization

The team says it is focused first on improving the user experience and proving incremental value for brands, while testing a generous free tier and paid tiers before settling on a longer-term commerce model.

Quotes:

> We're actually really focused on how to improve the user experience right now. ... We launched a subscription model out of the gates. The free tier is very generous.

### ✅ Verified WhatsApp identities can strengthen brand trust

A verified WhatsApp account can help travelers distinguish an official hotel or travel brand agent from an untrusted or impersonated account.

Quotes:

> You guys know that's really the WhatsApp account for that hotel, and that's really the WhatsApp account for travel, which is a very interesting thing that other folks are struggling with.

### 🌍 Muse reached 10 million users and is expanding internationally

Muse reportedly reached 10 million users in the US and Canada within weeks of launch, while expanding to Japan, Mexico, Singapore and Australia.

Quotes:

> We announced in earnings that 10 million people in the US and Canada are using Muse. And that's within weeks after launch. ... In recent weeks, it launched in Japan, Mexico, Singapore, Australia.

### ⚠️ AI frequently misstates flight offer details

Current AI agents struggle to accurately explain what airfares and offers include, especially when rules, contracts and NDC details are involved; one study cited on the panel found only about 6% accuracy across tested origin-destination questions.

Quotes:

> If you ask multiple AI agents the same question about different O&Ds all over the world and then compare the accuracy of the responses to what was actually filed, AIs only get it right about 6% of the time.

### 🚗 Uber uses agents to re-engage both sides of its marketplace

Uber primarily uses the agent to bring riders and drivers back to the platform, with reported improvements in re-engagement versus SMS.

Quotes:

> They've seen 27% improvement in rider re-engagement, but a 16% improvement in driver re-engagement versus SMS.

### 🧠 Connected data and memory enable proactive personalization

Connecting email, loyalty accounts, credit cards and historical trip data helps Muse build a richer understanding of the traveler. Its memory can then support recommendations and proactive reminders based on preferences and personal context.

Quotes:

> I have it connected to my email, my loyalty accounts, credit card. ... It knows what kind of car I drive. It knows I have 3 kids. It knows what airline I fly typically, what hotel I stay at. ... And then the second piece is memory. It knows the things that I care about.
