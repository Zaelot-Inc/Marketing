## Summary

Groupize pitched itself as a platform that helps corporations manage the spend, logistics, compliance, and travel for in-person meetings and events, with a particular focus on regulated industries such as life sciences and government, as well as guest travel. The pitch framed the core problem as the inefficiency of existing tools, which typically cap bookings at nine sleeping rooms, require 20 to 300 hours to run a meeting end to end, are still handled 80% in Excel, and must integrate with three to eight disparate corporate systems.

The addressable market was sized at $2 trillion, with groups, guests, and meetings representing 35% of the hotel industry; corporations were said to spend 1 to 5% of revenue on this category, ranging from $10 million for a typical corporation to $500 million for a life sciences company and $9 billion for the US government. The company noted it had won a Phocuswright innovation award 13 years earlier and has since built a business with roughly 120 marquee customers, many in regulated sectors, working alongside TMCs.

Eighteen months ago the company rebuilt its platform to be AI-native, centered on an orchestrating agent called Amy that coordinates 14 other agents to run meetings end to end, claiming 80–90% labor savings and 15–30% cost savings for corporations. MCP integrations are already in place, along with a first agent-to-agent integration with SAP. A live demo was planned showing a single prompt generating an event in Dublin for 50 new hires — including sourcing hotels and venues, building multilingual registration sites, booking room blocks and flights directly in Concur, and managing spend — though technical issues meant the accompanying video did not play as intended.

Revenue comes from corporate subscriptions and transaction fees, plus commissions, advertising, and an emerging payments line from a two-sided marketplace. On competition, Cvent was named as the main rival, expected to generate close to $1.5 billion in revenue this year despite only 5% market penetration, with the claim that Amy now outperforms it by being AI-native; newer entrants like BoomPop and Naboo were described as narrowly focused on sourcing, leaving the larger opportunity in shifting the market from Excel to agentic workflows. The team was positioned with deep domain experience — founders with 30 years in groups and meetings, a CTO who was ex-CIO of Boeing, and a head of sales who was ex-SVP at Concur — along with 13 years of integrations into hotel systems, OBTs, GDSs, and compliance systems, a presence across 75,000 corporations, and a newly received FedRAMP certification praised by the US government.

The company is raising a Series A to continue investing in product, integrations, and global go-to-market, and is now opening into the $2 trillion leisure market via white-label offerings for OTAs and event categories like weddings. In the feedback round, panelists called the problem real and the approach of applying deep domain expertise to an agentic model impressive, though one raised concern that incumbents with large existing corporate customer bases could move quickly to build similar AI capabilities, and another flagged that this could be a tough market from an investor standpoint even while agreeing the underlying problem and concept were sound.

## Key Takeaways

### 🛡️ Regulated industries are a core market

Groupize has built a customer base of roughly 120 marquee organizations, focusing on life sciences and the U.S. government. It says its deep compliance and enterprise integrations are reinforced by receiving official FedRAMP certification.

Quotes:

> We have some 120 marquee customers, big focus on regulated industry, the U.S. government... As of yesterday, we received our official FedRAMP certification.

### 📊 Meeting management is still largely manual

Despite the scale of the market, about 80% of meeting management is still handled in Excel, while a single end-to-end meeting can take 20 to 300 hours of work.

Quotes:

> It has to integrate to three to eight disparate systems in a company, and 80% is still done in Excel.

### 💳 Revenue comes from several transaction layers

Groupize’s business model combines corporate subscriptions and transaction fees with marketplace revenue from commissions and advertising, and it is expanding into payments.

Quotes:

> Groupize makes its money from subscriptions from the corporations and transaction fees, and also in our two-sided marketplace.

### 🔗 Enterprise connectivity is central to the platform

Groupize says it has MCPs in place and its first agent-to-agent integration with SAP in market, while the product also connects with systems such as Concur and existing corporate infrastructure.

Quotes:

> We actually have our first A to A with SAP in market.

### 🏨 AI handles sourcing and attendee operations

The proposed workflow includes shopping and negotiating with hotels, venues and restaurants, then creating registration sites, event apps and check-in functionality with support for speakers, sessions and tickets.

Quotes:

> She will shop the venues, the hotels, the restaurants; she will negotiate with them; she will create a complete registration website.

### 🗣️ One prompt can initiate event planning

The demonstrated workflow begins with a natural-language request—for example, setting up a Dublin event for 50 new hires—after which Amy asks clarifying questions and proceeds based on the confirmed assumptions.

Quotes:

> One prompt to Amy will do what would take one week for a human to do.

### 💰 Meetings represent major corporate spend

The session describes meetings as a substantial spending category: corporations may allocate 1% to 5% of revenue, with examples including $10 million for a corporation, $500 million for a life sciences company and $9 billion for the U.S. government.

Quotes:

> Corporations spend 1% to 5% of their revenues on this category.

### 🏢 Corporate meetings remain highly complex

Groupize positions corporate meetings as an operationally complex category spanning logistics, compliance and travel, particularly for customer-facing events and regulated industries.

Quotes:

> There’s nothing simple about a corporate meeting.

### ⚙️ Automation targets labor and savings

Groupize claims Amy can reduce meeting-related labor by 80% to 90% and help corporations save 15% to 30%, though these are presented as company claims rather than independently verified results.

Quotes:

> She saves 80% to 90% of the labor; she helps corporations save 15% to 30%.

### 🤖 Amy orchestrates an AI-native workflow

After rebuilding its platform over 18 months, Groupize now uses Amy as a supervising agent that coordinates 14 other agents to manage the meeting process end to end.

Quotes:

> Amy is a super agent that orchestrates with 14 other agents under the hood to do the end to end.
