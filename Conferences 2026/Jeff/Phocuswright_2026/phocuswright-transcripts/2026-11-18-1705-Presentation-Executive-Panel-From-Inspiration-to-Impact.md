## Summary

The session opened with framing for why inspiring travelers is especially hard right now: geopolitical turmoil, a record El Niño causing weather disruptions to events like marathons and ice-skating, extreme-wealth effects reshaping luxury expectations, and the approach of AGI flooding the internet with even more content. Despite this uncertainty, people are not expected to stop traveling, so the challenge for destination marketers and creators is to break through the noise and help people find places that make them feel most like themselves.

Panelists from TikTok, Beautiful Destinations and Visit California shared examples of what has recently broken through. TikTok highlighted Singapore Tourism Board's full-funnel "Journey" campaign, which used gamification, livestreams and creators rather than just pretty imagery to turn viewers into participants. Beautiful Destinations pointed to a broader shift away from seasonal campaigns toward always-on, constant content, noting the industry is finally adapting the always-connected approach it has advocated since its own founding. Visit California described moving away from traditional campaigns toward content built around a single promise—quiet and reassurance—given how anxious travelers currently feel.

Discussion turned to overtourism and the risk that a single viral recommendation can overwhelm a quiet spot. Panelists argued that content and creators are actually an opportunity for dispersion, letting smaller destinations without big ad budgets gain visibility by using the right content on the right platforms, while large, saturated hotspots can redirect some attention to mom-and-pop or lesser-known places. Visit California tied this to sustainability directly, describing its Traveler Responsibility Code adopted after pandemic-era overcrowding in rural areas, creator briefs that build in sustainable-travel messaging, and the ongoing challenge of communicating closures like Highway 1 or chain requirements on Highway 80 without discouraging visitors from California's hundreds of other destinations. The upcoming Olympics was raised as a live test case: Paris's experience showed a net tourism loss from visitors staying away, and Visit California is already working to message early that California and LA remain open and safe, aiming to disperse visitors and offset a projected 20% business impact in the LA region.

On whether destinations can actually steer demand, panelists agreed social platforms now shape far more of the journey than top-of-funnel inspiration alone. Over 60% of consumers reportedly begin travel planning on social media, often discovering a destination before the brand communicates anything itself, and TikTok data shows 60% of US users say they visited a place because of content seen on the platform—figures Visit California echoed by noting 76% of travelers lock in a destination before planning begins, meaning safety and logistical education (like road closures) needs to happen at the inspiration stage, not after a decision is made. Panelists noted social is now used much further down the funnel too—for comparison and decision-making, such as sharing reels of hotel room types—compressing campaign timelines from weeks to days and requiring brands to react almost in real time, including countering inaccurate media narratives with fast, factual alerts.

Closing on AI-generated video, panelists agreed authentic, creator-led content still performs best and builds trust, but AI is valuable for solving content-volume problems—filling gaps for brands that can't produce enough real footage of every hotel room or route—and for quickly generating variations of proven real footage for paid media testing rather than requiring new shoots each time. Visit California confirmed it similarly uses AI to repurpose the same content clips with different messages rather than replacing original real-world production.

## Key Takeaways

### 🧭 Set practical expectations before destination lock-in

Because travelers may choose a destination before researching access or conditions, destinations should deliver practical guidance during the inspiration phase—covering closures, responsible behavior, timing and alternatives—rather than waiting until trip planning is underway.

Quotes:

> 76% of people start their travel planning after they do destination lock ... The education has to happen early at the inspiration phase.

### 🔎 Social discovery can drive actual visits and planning

Social platforms increasingly influence the full travel journey: travelers discover destinations there, decide where to go and use short video to research and compare options before visiting.

Quotes:

> Over 60% of consumers will start their travel journey in social media ... We're seeing much further down the funnel decisions being made researching planning comparison.

### 🤫 Travel content can sell relief, not just destinations

In an anxious environment, destination marketing can break through by addressing what people need emotionally—such as quiet, recovery and personal space—rather than focusing only on destination attributes.

Quotes:

> The biggest campaign that has broken through for us right now is the one that sort of promises silence. A little less about us and a little more about you. What do you want?

### 🤖 Use AI to scale content without losing authenticity

AI can help travel brands increase content volume, create catalog variations and adapt paid media after identifying what works. The panel stressed that the underlying destination or product footage should remain accurate, creator-led content is especially effective, and outputs should follow brand guidelines.

Quotes:

> Creator-led content will always do much better than any other content on the platform. But if you're going down to lower the funnel and you need your catalogs and you need volume of content, I think AI has done a lot.

### 📱 Relevant content can outweigh media budgets

For destinations and travel businesses with limited funds, matching useful, platform-native content to the right audiences can generate disproportionate reach through recommendation algorithms, reducing reliance on traditional advertising.

Quotes:

> If you know how to make the right kind of content for the right kind of platforms you're going to have a far outsized impact than having big advertising budgets. You don't need to rely on that.

### 🏟️ Use major events to disperse visitors regionally

For major events such as the Olympics, destinations should start communicating early, promote experiences beyond the host area and set expectations about access and alternatives. Regional dispersal can help avoid concentrated losses and pressure in the event area.

Quotes:

> For the Olympics, dispersal is critical. ... LA has California. We are working in concert right now to start the messaging to assure people that traveling to California is going to be safe and open.

### ⚡ Build an always-on, signal-led content operation

Travel marketers can move beyond seasonal campaign windows by continuously publishing content and using feeds, trends and audience behavior as live research. This can shorten the distance between an insight, an idea and execution from weeks to days.

Quotes:

> The amount of data and insight that you get from looking at your feeds, looking at the content that's trending and then using that to inform your strategy means that you can move from these campaign windows ... to a matter of days.

### 🎮 Full-funnel campaigns turn inspiration into participation

Singapore Tourism Board’s Journey campaign combined gamification, live programming and creators to move beyond attractive imagery and actively bring audiences into the travel experience.

Quotes:

> They didn’t just use pretty pictures and videos and say, ‘Come visit Singapore.’ They utilized gamification. They went on live. They used a lot of creators to bring the noise to the platform and actually bring the people to the experience.

### 📰 Use factual alerts to counter broad travel narratives

Destinations can respond to confusing or outdated media coverage with timely, factual updates about what is open, closed or changing, helping travelers make decisions based on current conditions.

Quotes:

> We have a travel alert that we work on ... it's very factual. This highway is open. This highway is closed. And a lot of times it's reversing media narrative.

### 🌱 Destination marketing must protect travel assets

Travel marketers have a responsibility to safeguard natural and cultural assets by setting expectations for responsible behavior, incorporating sustainability into creator briefs and directing visitors toward viable alternatives when climate-sensitive sites or routes are disrupted.

Quotes:

> We have a responsibility in the travel industry to make sure that we are safeguarding our travel assets for the future.
