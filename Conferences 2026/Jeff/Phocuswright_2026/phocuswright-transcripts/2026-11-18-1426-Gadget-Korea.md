## Summary

The session opened with a pitch for a Korea-focused travel service, framed around the gap between booking a trip and actually experiencing a destination once there. The presenter noted that while booking flights, rooms and tables is largely solved, four out of ten visitors to South Korea report frustration with local digital services and payments, often because of self-verification requirements tied to a Korean phone number or apps that are not foreigner-friendly. A concrete example was given of a visitor arriving in Korea and trying to book a restaurant reservation, only to be blocked because the app demands a Korean phone number for verification.

To solve this, the company described two products: Travel Mode and a new service called Koro. The business originated as a South Korean eSIM provider and is repositioning as a travel super app; once a traveler buys the eSIM and connects upon landing, they gain access to a bundle of local mini-apps that are otherwise hard for foreigners to use, plus partner perks such as discounts with Olive Young or duty-free retailers. For longer-term stayers in Korea, who face problems like being unable to get a local number or data, visa confusion, and housing scams, the company is introducing passport-based verification: travelers can scan a passport before arrival, undergo an identity check, and receive a Korean mobile number via eSIM that activates on landing, removing the need for a Korean-number verification step.

The team argued its differentiation comes from owning the underlying technology rather than relying on third parties: its own eSIM platform, its own mobile line, and close partnerships with Korean companies such as Catch Table, Olive Young and Shilla Duty Free. The roadmap includes a global launch of Travel Mode by the end of 2026, passport verification rolling out in early 2027, and a planned collaboration with Visa, alongside future help with housing and job searches for long-term stayers. The founding team's credibility was tied to backgrounds at Wi-Fi Dosirak (described as Korea's top pocket Wi-Fi company), Shinsegae (a leading Korean retailer), and prior experience at the United Nations for the Chief Strategy Officer presenting.

Audience feedback was mixed, with two green and two red reactions. One questioner asked whether a visitor on a postpaid phone, rather than a prepaid eSIM, could still access the app's features, since the eSIM is the delivery mechanism; the presenter confirmed access would still be possible, and the questioner noted interest from a destination marketing organization looking beyond Korea. Another questioner pressed on differentiation, pointing out that many eSIM providers already exist and could pivot to offer the same thing, and observed that the pitch had initially come across as just another eSIM company rather than a broader travel super app. The presenter clarified that the eSIM itself is not the innovation but merely one component of a larger super app aimed at making the foreigner experience in Korea seamless, a framing the questioner said had not been clear from the pitch.

On distribution, the presenter said the company currently relies on B2B partnerships, largely with OTAs and companies interested in building their own travel super apps, and that it is the market leader within South Korea, though most of its partnerships remain domestic rather than international; distribution outside Korea has mainly taken the form of API integrations and white-labeling. The session closed with thanks to the presenter before moving on to the next speaker.

## Key Takeaways

### 🧩 Travel mode bundles connectivity with local services

After connecting in Korea, travelers can access integrated local mini-apps designed to be more foreigner-friendly, rather than navigating services that previously offered limited support to international users.

Quotes:

> Once you connect and land in Korea you basically get a wide variety of local apps that are integrated into our own app that are foreigner friendly.

### 🎁 Partnerships add benefits beyond connectivity

The travel offering is intended to include partner perks such as Olive Young benefits and duty-free discounts, extending the product beyond an eSIM or data connection.

Quotes:

> You also get special perks and benefits from our partners, whether that's maybe the famous Olive Young ... or maybe it could be duty free discounts.

### 🤝 Distribution is driven by B2B partnerships

Gadget currently distributes mainly through partnerships with OTAs and other companies, using APIs and white-label arrangements rather than relying only on direct consumer acquisition.

Quotes:

> For now, we are doing a lot of just B2B partnerships ... most of our distributions have been through API or a lot of white labeling.

### 🧳 Long-term residents face connectivity and settling-in barriers

The speaker highlights a broader set of challenges for foreigners planning to stay in Korea, including lacking a phone number or data, waiting for residence cards, visa confusion and housing scams.

Quotes:

> One of the biggest issues that a lot of foreigners wanting to stay in Korea have is they have no number, no data ... they have a lot of visa confusion or sometimes the most famous housing scams.

### 🛂 Passport verification enables phone plans before arrival

Gadget’s planned new service will let long-term stayers verify their identity with a passport and receive a Korean eSIM phone number before landing, so service can be activated on arrival.

Quotes:

> You can now also get mobile phone plans from South Korea even before you land in Korea. You simply scan your passport ... and then we issue you a number as an eSIM that you can activate as soon as you land.

### 📱 Korean phone verification creates widespread digital friction

The speaker says four in ten visitors report complaints about digital services or payments; a common issue is being unable to book restaurants or use local platforms because they require verification through a Korean phone number.

Quotes:

> Every 4 out of 10 visitors to Korea have some complaints about our digital services or payments. The most famous one being ... self-verification through a Korean number.

### 🗺️ The roadmap expands from travel into settlement support

After launching Travel Mode globally and rolling out passport verification, Gadget says it plans to support longer-term visitors with visa matters, housing, job searches and other settlement needs.

Quotes:

> Afterwards, we'll be working with visa issues, helping with housing, job searches, and much more.

### 🔗 Gadget’s differentiation rests on owned infrastructure and local ties

The company says its competitive advantage comes from technologies it already owns, including its eSIM platform and mobile line, combined with relationships with Korean businesses such as Catch Table, Olive Young and Shilla Duty Free.

Quotes:

> We run on technologies that we already own ... our own eSIM platform ... our own mobile line ... [and] work closely with a lot of local Korean companies.

### 🚀 Gadget is expanding from eSIMs into a travel super app

Gadget, originally a South Korean eSIM company, is transitioning toward a travel super app that brings connectivity and local services together.

Quotes:

> Gadget is originally a eSIM company from South Korea but we are now transitioning into a travel super app.

### 📲 The app is not limited to Gadget eSIM users

Gadget said travelers using a postpaid phone can still access the app and its local-service context; the eSIM is a delivery mechanism, not the only route into the experience.

Quotes:

> If I'm visiting Korea with a postpaid phone, not a prepaid SIM, can I still access the app? ... Yes, you can.
