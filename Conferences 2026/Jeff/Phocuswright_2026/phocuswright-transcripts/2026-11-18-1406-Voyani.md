## Summary

The pitch opened by framing a problem familiar to travel sellers: after the initial booking, travelers go on to spend significant money with other providers on dining, tours, transportation and events, money the original seller never captures, while customer support calls triggered by in-trip problems (flight delays, lost confirmations) eat into already thin margins. A cited study found 93% of travelers can't fully relax on a trip because managing logistics is their top stressor, and booking margins are further squeezed by massive customer-acquisition spend, with the four largest OTAs spending $20 billion on sales and marketing last year and Expedia putting half its revenue toward it.

Voyani's answer is to target the post-booking, in-trip window, pointing to research showing 47% of trip spend happens in-trip and that tours, activities and attractions alone were a $271 billion category last year. The product is positioned as a personal travel manager app that consolidates all of a traveler's confirmations, vouchers and itinerary details in one place, eliminating the need to dig through emails or scattered apps for things like museum tickets or rail passes, and reducing the support burden on the original seller because travelers already have answers in their pocket. Voyani acts as merchant of record on in-trip purchases made through the app (activities, eSIMs, local transportation, etc.) and shares a portion of that commission back with the partner who referred the traveler, at no cost or fee to the partner or the traveler.

Integration was described as lightweight: sellers keep selling what they already sell, then add a small code snippet to their confirmation page that links to Voyani; when a traveler clicks it, everything just purchased auto-populates their itinerary in the app, and any additional in-trip spend generates a shared commission. The pitch noted that Booking Holdings recently announced a $100 million investment in a similar product (Vail), but argued that the large OTAs will build this for their own customers, leaving an opening for Voyani to serve small and mid-tier travel sellers who lack the resources to build such a platform themselves.

In the Q&A, panelists pressed on differentiation given other players building similar in-trip tools; the response reiterated that the target market is small and mid-sized sellers rather than the large players building in-house. On the mechanics of capturing in-trip spend, it was clarified that Voyani is the merchant of record on purchases made through its own in-trip offerings, so it can track and share revenue on those, but it cannot pay commission on purchases made outside its network, though such outside purchases (confirmations, vouchers) can still be ingested into the app for organization. Asked about competition, the team said it sees no direct competitors today, though card providers were floated as a possible future one. A follow-up noted that being the merchant for only certain categories leaves some spend uncaptured, and it was confirmed that Voyani's merchant role is limited to post-booking, in-trip purchases like additional activities, eSIMs and local transportation, not pre-booking or pre-trip spend. On traction, the product was said to be live in beta with two partners, with the conference being used to recruit more.

## Key Takeaways

### 🔗 A confirmation-page link activates the itinerary

Travel companies can add Voyani with a small code snippet on their confirmation page; when travelers click the link, the products already sold to them automatically populate their Voyani itinerary.

Quotes:

> You offer Voyani with a small snippet of code that adds a link to your confirmation page. When the traveler clicks that link, everything you just sold them automatically populates their itinerary within Voyani.

### 📱 Voyani acts as a traveler’s personal trip manager

The app is designed to accompany travelers throughout the journey, consolidating trip details and reducing the need to search through emails or remember logistics.

Quotes:

> Voyani is your traveler's personal travel manager. Voyani is the app they take with them on their trip to eliminate their stress.

### 🔁 Better trip management is intended to improve retention

Beyond direct revenue, Voyani argues that a smoother trip experience can make travelers happier and more likely to return and book again with the originating travel company.

Quotes:

> But there's something even more important. And that's a happy traveler that comes back and books with you again because they had a great trip experience.

### 📞 Traveler anxiety creates costly support demand

When travelers struggle with logistics, they contact the company that sold the booking, adding support costs to already thin booking margins.

Quotes:

> And what happens when they're stressed? They call you. And every one of those anxious calls eats into what you already made on the booking.

### 📈 Partner revenue comes without additional acquisition cost

The pitch to travel companies is that Voyani can add revenue after the booking without requiring them to incur another customer acquisition expense.

Quotes:

> It's additional revenue without the cost. With your bookings, after acquisition costs and support costs, only a fraction of every dollar reaches your bottom line. From Voyani, every dollar goes straight there.

### 🧭 Post-booking experience is Voyani’s core focus

Voyani has spent the last 18 months building around the post-booking and in-trip experience rather than the initial reservation.

Quotes:

> While most of the industry has been focused on the initial booking, we've spent the last year and a half focused on everything after.

### 🎯 Voyani targets smaller travel sellers

Voyani is aimed at small and mid-sized travel sellers that cannot justify or afford the large investment required to build a comparable post-booking product themselves.

Quotes:

> Our target market is the small and mid-tier travel sellers who don't have the $100 million to invest in a product like this.

### 💳 Voyani monetizes in-trip purchases as the merchant

Voyani acts as the merchant for additional products purchased through its platform and shares resulting revenue with the travel company that originally connected the traveler. Travelers can also import externally purchased confirmations, but those purchases do not generate commissionable revenue for the originating seller.

Quotes:

> Anything that they buy within their trip on Voyani, we're the merchant, we track that, and that's how we share that revenue back with our partners.

### 🧪 Voyani is in beta with two live partners

The product has launched in beta with two partners so far, and the company is using the conference to recruit additional travel sellers.

Quotes:

> We're in beta live. So we have two partners today. And, you know, we're using this conference to help many more after this.

### 🛍️ In-trip spending is a large untapped opportunity

Voyani targets the 47% of trip spending that occurs in-trip, including activities, visas, eSIMs, local transportation and other services.

Quotes:

> A study by the National Travel Center found that 47% of spend on a trip happens in-trip. We capture that spend.
