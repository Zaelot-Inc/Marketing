## Summary

The panel opened with research framing showing a surge of consumer interest in AI and agents as travel-planning tools, but with booking volumes and trust still lagging behind the hype. This raised the long-running question of whether agentic AI will finally boost direct bookings or simply create a new, more fragmented surface area for intermediaries—echoing how both the internet and mobile eras were expected to favor direct booking but instead strengthened OTAs and increased distribution complexity and cost.

A hotel brand perspective laid out three “no-regret” investment areas meant to support more direct bookings regardless of how consumer behavior evolves: a modern, controllable technology stack that can plug into any emerging AI experience; granular, atomized inventory data (room attributes, exact locations, add-ons) so agents can match customer intent precisely; and active partnerships with AI platforms (Google AI mode, ChatGPT, Claude) plus a proprietary AI stay planner. Alongside this, owner-cost-reduction programs like RISE and the scale of the Honors loyalty program (270 million members) were presented as credible signals that increase an agent’s likelihood of recommending the brand, reinforcing trust and direct booking potential.

A marketplace perspective countered that leisure travelers rarely have strong brand loyalty and that a large-scale aggregator could in theory replicate much of what a major chain does, but across millions of properties, making scale and selection—not brand—the key competitive advantage; the view expressed was that “many middles will go away” while big brands and big aggregators both survive, leaving mid-sized intermediaries most exposed. This was tied to the aggregator’s strategy of onboarding boutique and branded hotels with competitive margins and aligned incentives, converting its large base of loyal, direct-booking travelers who have never stayed in a hotel into incremental demand for hotel partners, including via sub-distribution deals such as with American Express.

An owner/operator perspective, representing a mix of branded and independent properties, framed the marketplace relationship as an opportunity rather than a threat, since it converts a new generation of Airbnb-only travelers into hotel guests and helps surface unique, hard-to-sell inventory (one-off rooms, unusual layouts) that traditional OTA scroll formats bury. This depends on owners doing the backend work—correct attribution and structured content—so agents can discover and book these unique products; both the brand and platform speakers agreed that richer content and attributes let agents match guests to the “perfect” property rather than defaulting to generic price/location/review comparisons, potentially increasing willingness to pay. However, it was noted that better discovery does not guarantee a direct booking, since an agent may still route the booking through whichever channel or price point it finds most attractive, and price remains one of the strongest signals of intent in existing data.

## Key Takeaways

### 💰 Price remains a decisive test for agentic distribution

Even if agents improve matching and surface more distinctive hotel inventory, the benefits may be competed away when travelers prioritize price. The panel questioned whether agentic commerce will create incremental demand or simply redistribute the same stays toward the lowest-priced option, making owner economics and distribution margins critical.

Quotes:

> What we’ve seen in the data time and time again is one of the top elements of intent is price. And so how does that accrete or how does that show up in this new world?

### 🔄 Airbnb can bring incremental hotel demand

Airbnb argues that its hotel offering can deliver genuinely incremental guests: travelers who came to Airbnb intending to book an Airbnb may be converted to a hotel stay, creating value for participating hoteliers.

Quotes:

> Our guests... go direct, overwhelmingly direct, to the app. We're bringing an Airbnb guest through an Airbnb channel that went there originally to book an Airbnb, which has never meant a hotel until now.

### 🧱 Hilton’s first no-regrets priority is its technology foundation

Hilton’s initial investment area is its technology stack: a modern, cloud-based platform that the company controls and can evolve through its own development roadmap.

Quotes:

> Our technology platform... is modern, nimble, cloud-based, wholly under our control. We control the development roadmap. That includes PMS and CRS and everything around that.

### 📉 AI could lower guest-acquisition costs

Panelists expect total leisure guest-acquisition costs to be lower in five years, after the industry completes the current groundwork. The potential savings depend on better direct bookings, more efficient digital matching and distribution that remains profitable for owners.

Quotes:

> I think it’s a little more expensive right now as we’re laying the groundwork, but I think once you get the groundwork correct, it will lead to better direct bookings and lower overall costs.

### 🔎 Structured, granular content is essential for agentic discovery

Hotels need to atomize inventory and structure their content so agents can understand precise attributes, room locations, amenities, rates and distinctive property characteristics, then match them to a traveler’s intent. Availability alone is not enough if the property is not discoverable and bookable by the agent.

Quotes:

> It’s one thing to say this is great. I have a three-bedroom house... But have I done enough back-end work that it is discoverable and it is bookable?... We’re making substantial investments in our content and the structure of that content.

### ⚔️ AI may reward value-adding intermediaries and punish friction

The panel does not expect agentic distribution to eliminate intermediaries or guarantee direct bookings. Instead, AI could favor global brands and intermediaries that improve the customer or owner experience, while disadvantaging players that add friction, obscure choices or operate at uneconomic distribution costs.

Quotes:

> I think the future of AI distribution will be helpful for the big global brands and the intermediaries that are adding value to customers or to owners... What it will be unhelpful to is intermediaries that add friction or are deceptive or just create a problem in our ecosystem. I think AI will be an equalizer in that respect.

### 🔎 The key question is discovery versus integrated booking

The industry has not resolved whether AI agents will primarily provide a new surface for travel discovery or become an end-to-end booking tool integrated into commerce.

Quotes:

> There is some question about whether or not this is just a new surface area for discovery. Or is it really going to become an integrated booking tool?

### 🏅 Loyalty status could act as a trust signal to booking agents

Hilton believes its large Honors membership base can influence future agents: recognizing that a traveler is an Honors member may make the agent more likely to select Hilton.

Quotes:

> That sends a level of trust to an agent. It’s a highly credible signal to an agent. If that agent understands that you're an Honors member, that agent is going to book that brand, book a Hilton.

### 🌐 Hilton is connecting to multiple AI booking environments

Rather than waiting for one dominant AI interface, Hilton is working across major platforms and has launched an agentic stay-planning experience on its own direct site.

Quotes:

> We have a booking experience that allows shopping, booking, comparison in Google AI mode. We have a shop and book comparison tool in ChatGPT and in Claude... And then finally, we've launched an AI stay planner on our direct site.

### 🏗️ Scale may determine who wins in agentic distribution

Large platforms with broad supply can spread technology and merchandising investments across millions of properties and users. The panel suggested this may advantage scaled aggregators and make life harder for undifferentiated players in the middle.

Quotes:

> The ubiquitousness to be able to deliver that in many different places... with technology, could ultimately keep... a Hilton or a Marriott... doing well... But there’s a big world of others out there... Many middles will go away.

### 🤝 Better guest experiences protect owner economics

Regardless of how agentic distribution develops, delivering a strong on-property experience remains a durable competitive advantage. Hilton’s RISE program links lower owner program fees to achieving a specified stay-score threshold, aligning experience quality with owner profitability, loyalty and demand.

Quotes:

> We are... fueling the flywheel of owner profitability, customer experience, loyalty, and demand... RISE... is driving down a meaningful reduction in owner program fee if the owner is achieving a particular stay score threshold.

### 🤖 Agentic AI is attracting strong interest, but booking adoption lags

Travelers are showing substantial interest in AI as a planning tool and in agentic experiences, but that interest has not yet translated into comparable booking volumes. Trust remains a significant barrier.

Quotes:

> We're seeing a great deal of interest from the right kind of travelers. We're [not] seeing the booking volumes yet. And we're still seeing a pretty significant lag in trust.
