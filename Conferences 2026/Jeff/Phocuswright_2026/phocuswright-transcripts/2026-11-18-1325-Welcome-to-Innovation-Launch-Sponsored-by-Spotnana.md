## Summary

The session opened with an informal audience poll about whether an AI tool had helped attendees plan or book travel to the conference, with a show of colored cards indicating roughly half had used AI assistance. This led into anecdotes about the presenters' own journeys: one had used an AI tool to find a route from Singapore via Dubai to Miami but ultimately booked with a human agent out of a wish to support people in the industry, and recounted that Dubai airport at 1 AM was still packed with travelers buying dates, chocolate, and gold, suggesting that despite rapid change in travel technology, some human behaviors remain constant.

The discussion turned to how the travel startup landscape has shifted since the stage was known as the Travel Innovation Summit 18 years ago, noting that some of the sharpest new companies now come from growth markets in Asia, Latin America, the Middle East and Africa, where small teams can build and globally launch products quickly. It was emphasized that access to Silicon Valley funding is no longer the key competitive advantage; speed and customer insight now matter more.

This context framed the announcement of the Global Startup Pitch, a program run jointly by WIT, Phocuswright and Phocuswire and supported by American Express GBT, now in its fifth year with close to 1,000 entrepreneurs having applied globally. The program's first winner, Wheel the World, was cited as a success story, having since raised $19 million and demonstrated the scale of opportunity in accessible travel. The call for entries for the sixth edition, the Global Startup Pitch 2027, was formally opened, with attendees encouraged to share the program's QR code with founders who might belong on the stage.

Historical performance data for the Launch stage was shared to establish its credibility: since 2008, 682 companies have pitched, 527 of them (more than three in four) have gone on to raise funding totaling $14.7 billion, and 150 have been acquired, representing more than one in five presenters. It was noted that some of the acquirers of past presenters were in the room, and that the newly released Hot 25 Travel Startups list, sponsored by American Express GBT, already shows Odesia tagged as acquired following OpenAI's announced acquisition last week. Two companies from that Hot 25 list, Definitiv and Pintours, were flagged as presenting on the Launch stage that day, inviting the audience to judge whether the editors' picks proved accurate.

The format for Launch, presented by Spotnana, was then explained: companies of any size get six minutes to show a new product and must answer the single question of why now, after which a focus group of sales, strategy, investment and technology executives questions each presenter and rates how likely the product is to succeed. The audience was instructed to vote using smiley or frowny cards after each pitch and to cast a people's choice vote through the conference app, with three awards to be given — a focus group choice winner and runner-up, and a people's choice winner. The focus group panel was introduced, including representatives from Madrona Ventures and Spotnana, Perk, and American Express GBT, before the first presenter, from Fareportal, was welcomed to the stage.

Quotes:

Access to Silicon Valley funding is no longer viewed as a lasting competitive advantage; execution speed and a deep understanding of customers matter more.

Quotes:
