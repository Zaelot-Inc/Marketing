## Key Takeaways

### 🏗️ Buy broadly, build where proprietary advantages matter

Choice's framework is to buy AI capabilities such as agent platforms when vendors offer greater scale, governance, and tooling, while building in areas where proprietary data and workflows create differentiation, such as revenue management.

Quotes:

> For the large part, we're mostly going to buy, because there are plenty of organizations out there that have the scale, the governance, the tools. ... There are instances where we have something unique or proprietary that will benefit us.

### 🔧 Workflow redesign separates AI leaders

The biggest reported difference is whether companies have rebuilt core workflows around AI: 92% of those that did report major positive impact, compared with 40% of other companies.

Quotes:

> Among all companies, 92 percent of the ones that rebuilt core workflows report major positive impact from AI. Everyone else, 40 percent. That is a huge difference.

### 🚧 Modernization must happen without disrupting the core business

Travel companies are rebuilding incrementally while keeping critical transactions and customers running. Travelport migrated about 95% of customers to a consolidated platform over two years, while Evan rebuilt its stack during a carve-out and continued serving 40,000 properties.

Quotes:

> It's not just us deploying new technology. It's also our customers that needs to move to a new platform. ... over the last two years, we probably migrated now 95% of our customers to the new platform.

### 🤖 Agentic AI adoption is accelerating

Forty-five percent of surveyed companies are scaling or have scaled agentic AI, up from 28% in 2025. Startups are further ahead than established companies, at 54% versus 33%.

Quotes:

> So 45 percent of the companies that we surveyed are scaling or have scaled agentic AI. Up from 28 percent in 2025. And startups are driving this, being pretty far ahead at 54 percent. Versus established companies at 33 percent.

### 🧑‍💻 Adoption starts with useful workflows, not AI training

Employees are more likely to embrace AI when it removes a difficult, disliked task and delivers immediate value than when organizations mandate generic training. Experienced staff remain important as the judgment layer that can recognize when an AI-generated answer is wrong.

Quotes:

> When people have picked a very complicated process that everybody hates, and have automated it through an agent, that's where it clicks. ... The powerful thing about legacy knowledge and employees is they're really acting as the semantic layer ... providing the critical thinking that knows whether or not the agentic workflows are providing the right answers.

### 🛒 Travel companies lag travelers on agentic booking

Travelers are more prepared than travel companies for agentic commerce: 43% of U.S. travelers familiar with AI say they would book inside an AI platform, while only 28% of surveyed companies are actively working or planning to become bookable there. Technical interoperability is improving, with companies running model-context or agent-to-agent protocols in production rising from 9% to 31% in a year.

Quotes:

> 43 percent of U.S. travelers familiar with AI say they would book travel inside an AI platform. ... But only 28 percent of the companies we surveyed say they are actively working to build or planning to be bookable inside AI.

### 🔄 Self-improving loops connect measurement to revenue

Only 27% of surveyed companies have built self-improving AI loops. These loops depend on measuring outcomes and improving from them, and companies with them are more than twice as likely to quantify AI-driven revenue lift: 39% versus 16% without them.

Quotes:

> The most important part of the loop is step two, that AI can measure the result and figure out how to improve from there. ... Without them, it's 16 percent. With them, a whopping 39 percent that can quantify revenue lift.

### 🧹 Data, migration and change management are the hardest rebuild challenges

Rebuilding an AI-first stack while continuing to serve customers is difficult less because of AI tooling than because of legacy data, data cleaning, platform migration, employee education and process change. These constraints determine whether AI outcomes are reliable and whether teams can adopt new workflows.

Quotes:

> The data is really the biggest challenge, around having outcomes that you can rely upon. Cleaning the data. ... I also [see] the change management aspects. ... that's a lot of education. It's a lot of change in their processes.

### 🧮 AI advantage is a multiplication formula

The speaker frames AI advantage as multiplicative rather than additive: strength in one area cannot compensate for weakness in another, so companies must perform well across every variable.

Quotes:

> This is a multiplication formula, so strength on one variable doesn't make up for weakness on another. You've got to be good at all of them.

### 🎯 AI outcomes need formal KPIs

Although 74% of surveyed companies say leaders are personally accountable for AI outcomes, only 48% set formal KPI targets, limiting their ability to measure and improve what AI delivers.

Quotes:

> 74 percent of the companies we surveyed say leaders are personally accountable for AI outcomes. But only 48 percent set formal KPI targets for it.
