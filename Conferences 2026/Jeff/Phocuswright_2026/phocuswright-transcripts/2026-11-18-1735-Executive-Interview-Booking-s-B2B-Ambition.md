## Summary

The discussion opened with the rationale for unifying Booking Holdings' B2B operations into a single division despite the company's longstanding practice of keeping its brands independent. The reasoning centered on a large and growing B2B market where partners consistently voice two needs: help navigating tech disruption and demand for heavy customization rather than one-size-fits-all solutions. Concentrating energy on go-to-market and, especially, on product and technology was framed as the way to deliver explainable yet highly customizable tech to partners.

On scope, advertising on Booking's platforms is generally treated as part of the B2B remit, while payments is considered more complicated and was described as a separate, longer conversation, partly because some brands such as Agoda already run their own platform outside the Booking Partner Division's remit. Priceline's B2B business was explicitly excluded from the new division because of an ongoing FTC investigation and the operational complexities that entails; for now Priceline Partner Services remains separate with its own challenges, with no commitment to future integration.

Superior tech in B2B was defined as a combination of shipping speed, the ability to customize quickly at scale (a discipline learned from operating across diverse Asian markets), and staying state of the art with technologies like AI, aiming for a “best in the world” bar on each feature. Rather than favoring one platform, the approach draws on strengths from both Agoda and Booking.com technology depending on the product: APIs are leaning more toward what is now called the Booking Partner Division API (the former Agoda API), while other products, such as redirect-type integrations, lean on Booking.com, with most current production still running on Booking.com technology. Ahead of the January go-live, the team already largely operates as one unit with a playbook for cross-selling products, though a long tail of work remains around legal entities, financial systems and backend integration; the playbook itself was credited to a wider team effort and described as intentionally short and simple.

On agility within a large organization, B2C traffic was described as the proving ground: AI assistants and other products are tested and refined on consumer brands before being extended to B2B partners, so B2C and B2B are seen as mutually reinforcing rather than in tension. Demand for B2B travel services was characterized as broad across geographies and products, with financial institutions, airlines, and a growing stream of loyalty partners cited as attractive, and AI platforms positioned as partners to supply with Booking's underlying AI “brain” rather than competitors to fight, since consumers may reach that brain through various front ends. Despite the B2B push, B2C was described as an equally loved “kid,” with AI expected to expand B2C opportunity too, so the moves were framed as additive rather than a shift of priority away from consumers.

On competitive positioning, acquisitions remain a tool but are not the default, given confidence in building tech internally and an entrepreneurial culture inside Booking Holdings, with acquisitions more likely to fill capability gaps or open new verticals; AI was also credited with making tech cheaper and faster to build. Asked about industry comparisons on room-night production, the response acknowledged a feature-by-feature gap in places but expressed confidence that within six to twelve months each major product line could reach “best in the world” status. On a personal note, multiple AI agents are already used for everyday transactions like e-commerce and bookings, though not yet for high-stakes purchases, with experimentation framed as essential to learning. Success for the division in a year's time was defined as growth for partners and for Booking, and not losing partners to competitors on product capability; failure would be losing on tech rather than on price. Asked about a hypothetical life beyond Booking, interest in healthcare innovation and its potential to extend human lifespan was raised as the one area that might draw attention away from travel.

## Key Takeaways

### 🚧 The B2B operation is largely ready, with backend work remaining

The team already operates largely as one and has identified its product investments and commercial playbook. Remaining work is mainly a long tail of legal entities, financial-system integration and other backend services before full operation.

Quotes:

> By and large we are ready to operate from January. There is a long tail of creating more entities. There's a long tail of better merging financial systems.

### 🎯 Booking is concentrating B2B resources around customization

Booking believes B2B is a large opportunity, but partners increasingly need help navigating technological disruption and demand heavily customized solutions. It is therefore concentrating go-to-market, technology and product resources in a dedicated B2B effort.

Quotes:

> We felt that now is a good time in order to achieve that, to just focus our energy both on the go-to-market, but even more importantly on the tech and product, to really put technology that is explainable, simple, but highly customizable.

### 🌐 Demand spans markets, products and new partner types

Booking sees B2B growth opportunities broadly rather than in one geography or product category. Alongside established partners such as airlines and financial institutions, it is targeting newer travel distributors, particularly loyalty programs that want to add travel services.

Quotes:

> The market is so big pretty much everywhere ... Obviously you have the usual suspects, the airlines, the financial institutions ... but really everywhere we look we see opportunities to grow with our partners. Also a bunch of new partners that we can work with, especially loyalty.

### 🏢 Priceline B2B remains outside Booking Partners for now

Priceline B2B will stay separate because its operational challenges differ from those of the Booking Partners division, including complexity associated with an FTC investigation.

Quotes:

> We felt that the right thing to do is to keep those things separated. So for the time being Priceline B2B stays.

### 🧪 B2C brands will build and validate Booking’s AI before B2B rollout

Booking intends to build trusted, verticalized travel assistance in its consumer brands first, using real traffic to refine capabilities such as itinerary planning, booking and proactive support. It will then extend the proven experience to B2B partners rather than treating B2B as a replacement for B2C.

Quotes:

> We’re strong believers in verticalized travel assistance ... We’re definitely going to build it in B2C ... and because it’s going to be so great, we’re going to extend it to the B2B side.

### 🧬 Booking and Agoda technology will be combined selectively

The unified B2B operation will not standardize on one technology stack. API products will lean more heavily on Agoda’s former API, while other products—such as redirect experiences on Booking.com—will use Booking technology.

Quotes:

> It's really a mixture. There are a bunch of products when it comes to API there will be more will be leaning more into the booking partner division API that is the former Agoda API. But there are other products where we are leaning for instance ... redirect type of products ... that will lean heavily on booking.

### 🔄 B2B expands Booking’s reach rather than replacing B2C

Booking does not view B2B as a superior alternative to B2C. Its strategy is to serve consumers through whichever brands, partners or interfaces they choose, accepting some overlap because the opportunity to reach consumers elsewhere is larger.

Quotes:

> We go to the consumer where the consumer is ... The potential to serve the consumers elsewhere is much bigger than trying to worry too much about the overlap.

### ⚡ Superior B2B tech means speed, customization and simplicity

Booking defines superior B2B technology through rapid shipping, scalable architecture and the ability to customize quickly for different markets and partners. It also wants solutions to remain simple and explainable while incorporating state-of-the-art capabilities such as AI.

Quotes:

> Superior tech means agility. Being able to customize. And then being state of the art with the newest technologies like AI.

### 🤖 AI platforms can access Booking’s travel intelligence

Booking sees AI platforms as another interface to the same consumer-facing travel capability, not merely as channels for distributing inventory. Its ambition is to provide itinerary planning, booking, recommendations and proactive support through partners as well as its own brands.

Quotes:

> Eventually we want them to talk to our AI brain. The same AI brain that can travel in itinerary for you, help you book, make recommendations, be proactive when things change.

### 🏆 Booking aims to close B2B feature gaps within 12 months

Booking sees B2B as a feature-driven competition. While acknowledging that some capabilities still need improvement, it believes focused investment and its technology platform can put each major product line in a best-in-world category within roughly a year.

Quotes:

> B2B is a feature game ... with the right focus, certainly with the type of tech that you have today, there’s no reason why 12 months from now we cannot look at each one of the main product lines ... and tell yourself we are in this ‘best in the world’ category.

### 💸 AI efficiency comes from matching models to tasks

Booking is testing multiple AI models and selecting the right one for each use case rather than relying on a single premium model. It sees this task-specific approach as a way to control token costs while retaining capability.

Quotes:

> We optimize that cost by picking the right model for the right task. On average you don't need to pay the premium cost.

### 📈 B2B success means partner growth without product-driven losses

Morgenshtern defines success primarily through growth for B2B partners and Booking. He also wants the division’s technology to become best-in-world enough that partners do not leave because competitors offer capabilities Booking cannot match.

Quotes:

> BPD is about growth. It’s about growth for our partners. Growth for partners is growth for us ... I’m not willing to lose on product. In a year from now, that shouldn’t happen.
