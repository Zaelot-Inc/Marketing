## Summary

The session opened with the tail end of a prior panel discussion about airline distribution, where panelists debated why a travel technology was being positioned toward OTAs rather than travel management companies (TMCs), noting that TMCs typically access airline content through GDS and questioning whether a live partner outside of one specific carrier existed; the answer confirmed one live partner in Europe before the panel wrapped up.

The next speaker was introduced as representing Globaleur, which presented an agentic AI system designed to personalize, plan, sell, and settle an entire trip within a brand that travelers already use, rather than sending them to third-party platforms.

The core problem framed for the industry was that travel brands generate customer intent but third parties capture most of the resulting margin and transaction value. This was illustrated with market figures: airlines sold $157 billion in ancillaries in 2025, yet far larger adjacent categories—hotel stays, tours and activities, rental cars, and other ancillaries—are mostly captured outside of the originating brand. The tours and activities market alone was cited at $271 billion, with 67% of those bookings still happening offline, underscoring the scale of value leaking away from brands.

The system was described as being deployed directly inside airline platforms, hotel platforms, airports, and other partner brands. It first interprets traveler intent from natural-language requests (for example, a family trip to Tokyo for three days), then personalizes a plan using preference and demographic data, upselling stays, tours, activities, dining, and other experiences. Rather than requiring travelers to jump across five different platforms, the system settles all bookings within the host brand's own platform, and performance data is fed back to partner brands to continuously improve the experience.

Concrete use cases were walked through for different partner types. With airlines, the tool is embedded in websites, mobile apps, and airport lounges, leveraging existing booking data (traveling party, trip length, destination) so travelers can immediately chat with the agent about preferences such as dietary needs. With hotels, a QR code placed in guest rooms lets properties push offers and facility information while guests ask questions about amenities, hours, or airport transport, with the concierge handling responses. For destination marketing organizations, a partnership with one of Asia's largest DMOs, Busan, was highlighted: a previously static, blog-style 2D content platform was rebuilt into a fully agentic system that lets visitors specify interests—such as K-pop, beauty, or seafood—along with travel-party details to receive a complete, tailored Busan itinerary covering tours and activities. The presentation then moved toward explaining why major travel brands choose to partner with the company, comparing its offering to alternatives, before the transcript cuts off.

## Key Takeaways

### 🧳 Globaleur positions agentic AI as an end-to-end commerce layer

The platform is presented as an agentic AI system that can personalize a trip, plan the itinerary, sell related products, complete the booking, and settle the transaction within a travel brand's environment.

Quotes:

> It's an agentic AI system that personalizes, plans, sells, and settles your entire trip in a brand that everyone already uses.

### 🛍️ The platform cross-sells the full trip around the flight

After a flight is booked, Globaleur can recommend and sell stays, tours, activities, dining, and other trip components based on the traveler's profile and requirements.

Quotes:

> We upsell your stays, tours, and activities, places to eat, places to do, based on your traveler profile.

### 💰 Travel brands risk losing ancillary revenue to third parties

Globaleur's core premise is that brands generate travel intent but third-party platforms capture much of the resulting transaction value, especially beyond the flight booking.

Quotes:

> Travel brands create the intent, but the third party captures all the margins and transactions.

### 🗺️ Traveler intent becomes the starting point for personalization

A natural-language request—such as a three-day Tokyo trip with a child—provides the basis for tailoring the itinerary using preferences, demographics, and other traveler inputs.

Quotes:

> I'm flying to Tokyo for three days, with a child. And then our system starts personalizing based on your preference, demographics, and all the other inputs.

### 🗺️ Tourism boards can turn destination content into trip planners

Globaleur described replacing a static, blog-style destination platform with an agentic system that builds complete itineraries around visitor interests, travel companions, and trip length.

Quotes:

> While it used to be a blog, 2D, like, you know, content-based system, we totally overhauled their entire platform into an agentic system.

### 🏨 Hotels can add an AI concierge through room-based access

Hotels can place QR codes in rooms to let guests ask questions, receive personalized offers, learn about facilities, and arrange services such as airport transportation.

Quotes:

> Once you check in, you scan a QR code that we place in each of the rooms... As a guest, you can ask any questions. What are the pool hours? Do you have any special arrangements for me? How can I get to the airport?

### 📱 Airline touchpoints can extend beyond the website

The proposed deployment model places the agent across airline websites, mobile apps, and airport lounges, using existing booking data to begin post-booking trip planning.

Quotes:

> We usually sit in their website, mobile app, and airport lounges. So once you book the flight, we already have your travel data.

### ✈️ Airline content is expanding beyond traditional GDS channels

The discussion highlighted that TMCs typically connect to airline content through GDSs, while broader carrier support—including low-cost carriers—is an important opportunity.

Quotes:

> Usually the TMCs connect the airline content most through GDS... our pitch point here is we are able to handle all types of carriers. Low-cost carrier is a great example.

### 🏷️ Brands can consolidate fragmented travel bookings

Globaleur aims to let travelers book multiple trip components inside the brand's platform instead of being sent to several external services.

Quotes:

> Instead of going out to five different platforms, now you can book all of this within the brand's platform.

### 🥗 Conversational requirements can shape the itinerary

Travelers can communicate practical needs, such as dietary preferences, through the agent, which then plans the itinerary around those requests.

Quotes:

> You can start chatting with the agent, whether you're a vegetarian, all the other requirements that you possibly have. We'll plan the travel itinerary around those requests.
