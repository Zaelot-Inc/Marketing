## Summary

The panel brought together three of the world's largest OTAs—Despegar (Gonzalo), MakeMyTrip (Mohit) and Priceline (Brigit)—alongside OAG, which supplies flight data across the industry and acted as a neutral referee for the discussion. The conversation was structured around Phocuswright's 2026 themes of pressure, power and pivot, with audience polling via Slido and smiley/frowny cards used throughout to test agreement.

On pressure, the audience voted that AI assistants and agents represented the greatest threat to travel distribution, echoing a similar result from a prior WIT survey. Panelists, however, described different regional pressures: Priceline pointed to US affordability concerns driven by rising supplier costs and historically low consumer sentiment despite continued demand for travel; Despegar cited the sheer pace of change and acceleration, noting its AI agent already drives 8% of sales; MakeMyTrip highlighted pricing pressure on airlines tied to oil-price shocks from the West Asia crisis, offset by strong non-air growth (now two-thirds of its mix) in buses, cabs and bundled holidays; and OAG framed the overall picture as cyclical economic volatility compounded by overhyped expectations around agentic AI, arguing travel agencies won't simply disappear. OAG's capacity data showed flat or declining growth in markets like Brazil's domestic sector, steady conditions in North America, and strong double-digit international growth in India.

Discussion of OTA market share showed Phocuswright forecasting a dip to 47% of the online market in Latin America by 2028, which Despegar framed as healthy industry democratization and competitive growth rather than a threat, a view the audience largely endorsed via card vote. MakeMyTrip acknowledged air ticketing volumes are under pressure from capacity constraints, airline exits and the oil crisis, but said resilient growth of 20%+ is coming from non-air transport and bundled accommodation offers suited to different budgets.

On power, the audience again pointed to AI assistants and super apps as likely owners of the traveler relationship in five years. Priceline said it is investing both in third-party integrations (ChatGPT, Snapchat) and in its own AI assistant, Penny, insisting OTAs must be wherever customers are, while the audience voted confidently that US OTAs can still win in air. Capital and ownership questions surfaced around Prosus's stakes in both Despegar and MakeMyTrip rival Ixigo; Despegar said shared AI investment across Prosus portfolio companies does not involve sharing customer data, while MakeMyTrip noted its long public-market history has made capital access a non-issue, with competitive advantage resting on execution, supply depth and customer experience. OAG argued that whoever sits in the transaction—not necessarily the AI agent—holds the real power, and that agencies will likely survive AI disruption much as they survived prior shifts to Google and Meta.

## Key Takeaways

### 🌍 More competition could grow travel while reshaping OTA share

Low-cost carriers and more competitive distribution could make travel more affordable and expand the overall market, even if OTAs lose some booking share. The panel framed that as potentially positive for efficient distributors.

Quotes:

> For the industry it means democratizing travel, making it more affordable, and when travel grows actually it's good for us.

### 💳 The transaction intermediary may still own the traveler relationship

Despite predictions that AI assistants will control the customer relationship, the panel argued that whoever sits in the middle of the transaction—especially where the traveler pays—may retain that relationship. In either case, price, supply depth and the ability to combine experiences remain core competitive advantages.

Quotes:

> Anyone who is in the middle of the transaction where you put your money owns the relationship with the customer—and that happens to be the agencies at this stage.

### 📈 Latin America’s growth is accelerating OTA execution

Latin America was described as the fastest-growing region, with Despegar gaining market share and responding by launching products faster as customer and technology expectations change.

Quotes:

> We are gaining market share within that context. We are launching a lot of new things, shipping products faster than ever before.

### 🧠 Cross-category commerce models can power connected trips

Prosus is investing in a large commerce model trained on transactions across its portfolio, not just travel. By predicting a customer’s next action and transferring insights across categories, the model could help OTAs connect flights, hotels and other trip components in a more natural journey.

Quotes:

> It's basically trained on billions of transactions around all of the different portfolio companies, not only travel. And basically what it does is exactly predict the next action that someone will take in the path to eventually buying something.

### 🇮🇳 MakeMyTrip shifts growth beyond air

Air-ticketing growth in India is under pressure from capacity constraints and higher fuel costs, so MakeMyTrip is growing non-air businesses such as buses and outstation cabs, while bundling transport with accommodation for different budgets.

Quotes:

> A lot of the growth is coming in from non-air... bus ticketing with outstation cabs... What we're trying to do is bundling them up with right kind of accommodation options that can work for people.

### 🇮🇳 India leads international capacity growth

Flight-capacity data pointed to India as the standout growth market, with nearly double-digit international capacity growth and increasing low-cost-carrier capacity; Brazil’s domestic capacity was declining while North America remained broadly stable.

Quotes:

> The good story here is India. India is increasing capacity. It's growing... You can see almost double digit growth in terms of international.

### 🤖 AI agents could be either a channel or a travel product

The panel’s central strategic question is whether AI agents will merely distribute existing travel products or become products that combine experiences and reshape how trips are bought. Agencies may remain resilient if agents need deep supply, pricing and itinerary-combination capabilities.

Quotes:

> The bigger question for us to answer... is the AI agent a distribution channel or a product? If it is a product then the agencies will thrive because they've been doing it for many years.

### 💰 Affordability and geopolitical costs pressure travel

The panel described an unaffordability crisis driven by supplier operating costs, inflation, geopolitics and fuel prices. Consumers still want to travel, but discretionary spending is harder to sustain.

Quotes:

> I think the industry is experiencing a little bit of an unaffordability crisis. This is driven by geopolitics and like the cost base of a lot of the suppliers that we actually use in the industry.

### 🤖 AI agents already generate 8% of Despegar sales

Although the panel debated the pace and significance of agentic AI, Despegar reported that an AI agent already represents 8% of its sales, showing measurable commercial impact today.

Quotes:

> Today we have an AI agent that represents already 8% of our sales.

### 💬 Conversational agents must become richer than chat

OTA assistants such as Priceline’s Penny are being built to understand traveler intent and interact with other agents, moving beyond static APIs toward personalized recommendations. The panel also stressed that travel discovery needs visual and inspirational elements, not just a text-chat interface. Adoption remains early, with Penny still scaling.

Quotes:

> Please let's not have it just as a chat. I mean it needs to be a little bit more inspirational. Like we still need to see the hotel, the beach, the country and get inspired.

### 🔗 OTAs must meet travelers—and their agents—where they are

OTAs are investing in their own products while integrating with major LLMs and third-party platforms. They are also preparing to serve AI agents as customers, not just human travelers, so they can remain part of the transaction wherever it begins.

Quotes:

> We want to be wherever our customers are... that means with third parties, but that also means we want to serve whatever customer comes to us. So human customers, agents as customers.
