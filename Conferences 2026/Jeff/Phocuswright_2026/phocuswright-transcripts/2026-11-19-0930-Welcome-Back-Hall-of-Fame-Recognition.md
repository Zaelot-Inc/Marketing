## Summary

The discussion opened with a question about how the rise of AI agents making booking and offer decisions might ripple through the complex partnership networks that underpin loyalty programs, and what this means for how such partnerships should be structured going forward.

One view was that these partnerships would actually become stronger, particularly around servicing disruptions, because agents could operate across brands to solve problems that currently create friction. This was illustrated with a personal example of using an AI assistant (Muse) during a flight disruption: the tool rebooked a flight on a non-partner airline and adjusted hotel arrangements seamlessly, prompting the observation that if airline partnerships had been made visible and surfable to the agent, the disruption could have been handled entirely within the existing partner ecosystem, which would have been the ideal outcome.

Another perspective framed loyalty programs as marketplace builders: by combining demand with a virtual currency, these programs effectively create an ecosystem, making the choice of partners a critical strategic decision that shapes the entire marketplace going forward.

The conversation then turned to owned distribution channels, asking whether the growth of agentic bookings means travel brands are effectively giving up on their own websites and direct customer access points. It was noted that suppliers have already begun asking whether they even need a website anymore. The response was that owned channels are not being abandoned in the near term, but their role needs to be rethought: brands may need to make their own assets richer in content, reconsider booking flows, and rethink how customers experience those channels, since owned assets remain important for the foreseeable future even as agents take on more of the booking decision-making.

The session closed with a sponsor segment promoting a free research study, invited via a scannable code, examining how brands are showing up and what is being said about them, before thanks were extended to the panelists. The program then transitioned into the Hall of Fame recognition, introduced by recalling that the Phocuswright Hall of Fame was established in 2018 to honor individuals who played an instrumental role in shaping the travel industry by refusing to accept the status quo and driving new developments.

## Key Takeaways

### 🤝 Partnerships should enable complete agent-led servicing

The value of a partnership increasingly depends on whether its inventory and capabilities are accessible to an agent that can handle an entire customer problem, not just make an isolated booking.

Quotes:

> If those partnerships had made surfable to my agent that the disruption could be completely handled on them, that would have been ideal.

### 🌐 Owned websites remain important in the near term

The rise of agentic bookings does not mean suppliers should abandon their websites or other controlled customer access points; those assets are expected to remain important for the foreseeable future.

Quotes:

> We're certainly not there right now. In fact, you do need to think about your assets that you control differently.

### 🧭 Partnership strategy defines the customer ecosystem

Choosing partners is a strategic decision because it determines the boundaries and capabilities of the marketplace that a loyalty program can offer through agents.

Quotes:

> The notion of who you partner with is extremely important. Because you're defining really that ecosystem, that marketplace.

### 🏪 Loyalty programs can become partner marketplaces

Loyalty programs have the ingredients to organize a marketplace around their partners: existing customer demand, an ecosystem of participating brands, and a virtual currency.

Quotes:

> Loyalty programs have the ability to create a marketplace with their partners. They've got the demand. They've got a virtual currency.

### 🤖 Agents can resolve disruptions across brand boundaries

Travel agents may be able to solve servicing problems that span airlines, hotels, and other brands—for example, rebooking a disrupted flight on a non-partner airline and updating the hotel reservation as part of one recovery process.

Quotes:

> Muse went in, put me on a flight on a non-partnership airline, rebooked and redid everything on my hotel. And it was absolutely solved.

### 📝 Websites need richer content for agentic discovery

Suppliers may need to enrich the content on their owned digital assets so that customers—and potentially the agents acting for them—can understand and evaluate the offering more effectively.

Quotes:

> You may need to make them much richer in terms of the content that's within them.

### 🔄 Booking flows must evolve for new customer experiences

Beyond adding content, suppliers need to rethink how customers experience their sites and how booking flows work as agentic interactions become more common.

Quotes:

> You may need to think about the way a customer is going to experience it and the booking flows. So there's a lot of implications that may require rethinking.
