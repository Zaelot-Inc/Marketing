## Summary

The panel opened by framing a shared challenge: an airline, a credit-card travel business, and a membership organization for the 50+ community are all effectively selling travel to overlapping customers, raising questions about how each defines and targets its most valuable segment without excessive overlap. United described its most valuable customer as the loyal traveler who actively chooses the airline, acknowledging the definition is still evolving internally and may include travelers willing to take a connection to fly United, with segmentation built into cabin design from Polaris to Premium Plus to differentiated economy products like RelaxRow. AARP emphasized that its 50+ audience is not a monolith but a massive, $12.5 trillion economic group that will soon be the largest adult population segment in the US, requiring marketing by life stage rather than age, supported by data showing high digital device usage and rapidly doubling AI adoption among older travelers. Capital One Travel positioned its most valuable customer as the cardholder who uses card benefits and the travel portal together, noting these customers spend more, refer others, and are less likely to close their accounts.

Asked who 'wins' when a 55-year-old leisure traveler begins planning a European trip, United argued it does not need to be the starting point, noting it has no monopoly on inspiration and is happy to be found on TikTok, Expedia, or the Capital One portal as long as customers eventually convert. AARP said its edge lies less in being the starting point than in trust earned through decades of advocacy and vetting of partners, which gives members confidence that the whole trip, not just the booking, will go well. Capital One framed travel as a non-winner-take-all market, arguing that brand-loyal flyers will book direct while less loyal customers are drawn to its simplicity and flexibility across booking, lounges, and hotel collections, and that there is room for competitors to both compete and partner. A deeper dive into Capital One's strategy showed a significant share of Venture cardholders already using the card for travel even before the Discover acquisition, with the company explaining that owning its technology and agency end-to-end, including through the Hopper acquisition, lets it build trust, value, and simplicity across the full travel lifecycle rather than outsourcing to an OTA.

On AI agents booking directly into inventory, United confirmed it currently blocks this, citing concerns over customer data protection during an automated transaction rather than opposition to AI itself; it still welcomes AI-driven research and inspiration but wants a human in the loop for the actual purchase while it works out what it needs from partners to authorize specific agents safely. Capital One declined to comment on other companies' strategies but said account security is table stakes for a financial institution and that it is closely watching the trade-off between the ease AI agents offer and the trust issues they raise. It was noted that recent industry outages have not helped consumer confidence in automated booking, though comparisons were drawn to past hesitancy around entering credit cards online, suggesting trust in AI agents may build over time, while AARP added a cautionary note about AI's tendency to hallucinate even in research and planning contexts.

The conversation then turned to whether AI could help travelers optimize across competing loyalty programs and cards. AARP pushed back on the idea that consumers actively think about optimizing their loyalty portfolios, arguing instead that the real optimization consumers want is simply ease and savings, achieved through trusted relationships rather than point-juggling. United agreed but added a marketer's perspective, arguing that some AI-driven optimization use cases will emerge, but brands still need to cultivate an emotional connection that leads customers to choose them directly. Capital One suggested AI's broader role is to simplify complex decisions on a customer's behalf, predicting growth in customers asking AI agents how to best use their points and benefits.

The session closed on generational differences in loyalty drivers. United shared proprietary cross-generational research finding that reliable, free Wi-Fi was the one universal priority, which it tied to its Starlink investment as a modern, non-traditional loyalty lever. AARP offered a complementary view, noting that older generations respond more to company credibility, Gen X responds to word of mouth, and Gen Z cares about ethical corporate behavior, while stressing that inclusive design choices such as high-contrast, easy-to-read websites can welcome older users without alienating younger ones. Capital One closed by noting that its travel portal is actually seeing the most growth among younger, less brand-loyal Gen Z and millennial customers, who value the flexibility to earn and redeem value across many places rather than committing to a single airline or hotel brand.

## Key Takeaways

### 🛠️ Capital One is building end-to-end travel capabilities

Capital One is investing in its own technology and agency operations, including Hopper capabilities, to control the travel experience and create services tailored to cardholder needs—from booking through the trip.

Quotes:

> Fully owning our own technology end to end and owning our agency allowed us to provide them more value and build unique [experiences] for our cardholders' needs... We’re focused kind of holistically how we can make travel easier for our cardholders.

### 🧭 Consumers want loyalty made simple

Travelers generally are not trying to optimize every card, membership and portal in their wallet. The strongest proposition is to make it easy to save money, book confidently and receive relevant value, whether or not an AI agent eventually handles the optimization.

Quotes:

> I don't think people wake up thinking about all of their loyalty cards and how to optimize them... Any place I think where just make it easy for me to save money, make it easy for me to book. That's the optimization I believe for a consumer.

### 🔄 Generational change is reshaping the 50-plus audience

Travel marketers must prepare for a changing 50-plus customer base: Gen X is entering the segment now, and millennials will begin turning 50 within four years, bringing different planning, loyalty and travel behaviors.

Quotes:

> Gen X is now 46 to 61. And millennials will start turning 50 in four years. So sit on that for a minute, right? Very, very different practices and loyalty and planning and travel.

### 📱 Older travelers are highly digital

The assumption that older consumers are not digitally active is misleading: 90% of people over 50 have a cell phone, they use an average of seven digital devices, and AI usage doubled from 2024 to 2025.

Quotes:

> There's kind of this misnomer that older people aren't digitally active. And we know 90% of people over 50 have a cell phone. The average amount of digital devices... It's 7, right? ... in the last from 24 to 25, AI usage double.

### 👥 The 50-plus market is vast and diverse

The 50-plus audience spans a very wide age range and should not be treated as one homogeneous segment; it represents a $12.5 trillion economy and is on track to become the largest share of the U.S. adult population.

Quotes:

> The 50-plus is not one monolith of a consumer. And 50 to 100 is an absolutely enormous group of people... It's not a niche. It's the market.

### 🤝 Trust keeps human help relevant

AI is increasingly useful for travel planning, inspiration and research, but human assistance remains important when trips are complicated or require confidence and trust, such as cruises or multigenerational travel.

Quotes:

> While AI is gaining usage for planning and ideating, at the end of the day, trust sort of wins that relationship. And when things are complicated, maybe difficult to plan... that's where that in-person piece does come in.

### 🌐 Travel brands need an omnichannel presence

United does not expect to own the entire discovery journey; it aims to reach travelers wherever they seek inspiration or complete a purchase, including its own channels, Expedia and the Capital One portal.

Quotes:

> We don't have a monopoly on research and inspiration... But we want you to find our content wherever it is that you would like to make that purchase... through Expedia and, of course, through the Capital One portal as well.

### 🤝 Trust and vetting are AARP's relationship advantage

AARP uses the trust built through its broader advocacy and member work, together with vetting its commercial partners, to give members confidence that providers will support them throughout the trip—not just at booking.

Quotes:

> People who belong to AARP know that we're looking out for them and we're going to vet things and look at things so that they can have a good trip because it's not just about that first step... It's the whole thing.

### 🔐 AI booking needs safeguards

United is pausing direct AI-agent booking because agents may require access to sensitive customer data. The company is exploring standards and partner requirements that would make automated transactions safe while keeping humans in control for now.

Quotes:

> We absolutely still encourage the research and the inspiration and comparison... but it's just the booking part... We still want the human in the loop for that transaction.

### 📶 Practical benefits can drive loyalty

Loyalty is not limited to traditional brand attachment or points programs. Across generations, travelers may be won by practical benefits that consistently improve the journey—especially free, reliable Wi-Fi.

Quotes:

> The one thing that everyone cares about is good, free, reliable Wi-Fi. There are still levers to drive loyalty that may be in a non-traditional sense, but in the sense of delivering on what the traveler is looking for and what they value.

### 💳 Travel engagement strengthens card value

For Capital One, customers who use travel benefits and Capital One Travel are more valuable across the card relationship: they spend more, refer more friends and are less likely to close their cards.

Quotes:

> Customers that use the benefits of the card and use Capital One Travel, they tend to be much more valuable customers overall. They are higher spending. They're more likely to refer a friend to get a card. They're significantly less likely to close their card as well.

### 🎯 Segment broad markets by customer needs

Serving a broad population requires understanding customers' life stages, travel preferences and problems rather than relying on age alone, then developing the right offering for each segment.

Quotes:

> What we try to do is meet people where they are. And not understand and market to them by their age, but rather what life stage they're in, their chronology, what sorts of problems they're trying to solve for.
