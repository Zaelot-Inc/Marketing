## Summary

The pitch opened with a problem drawn from personal experience running a startup: every department wanted to travel for its own reasons, and all spending decisions about whether a trip was worth taking landed on one desk, with no good way to judge value against cost or to know afterward what the trip had actually accomplished.

Swivul was introduced as a travel and entertainment operating system built around an AI concierge called Sky, designed to close the gap between cost and value by connecting internal company data (CRM, calendar, preferences, goals) with external opportunities (conferences, customers, partners). Rather than waiting for someone to request a trip, Sky proactively surfaces travel worth taking, illustrated by a claim that it had identified millions in pipeline and multiple high-value meetings tied to the very conference where the pitch was delivered.

A demo walked through planning an F1 Miami trip, showing Sky identifying an unmentioned multimillion-dollar CRM opportunity and building an entire itinerary around closing it, sourcing passes, rooms, dinners, and transport across the GDS, connected APIs, and local concierges into a single checkout. After the trip, Sky was shown closing the loop by checking CRM and calendar data to see whether meetings happened and opportunities advanced, producing trip attribution without added work, and then rolling this up across the whole travel program to show which trips created pipeline, advanced deals, or retained customers, analyzed like an investment portfolio to guide future spend.

On the business model, Swivul takes a blended roughly 12% take rate on supplier margins and markups plus a company subscription, claims to move companies from intent to checkout five times faster than leading travel platforms, and says its differentiation beyond standard booking, approvals, and receipts lies in its intelligence layer connecting travel decisions to business outcomes. The company cited Mercury as a partner bringing its 300,000+ business customers, pointed to its own use of the product (Sky recommending a conference pitch that won a people's choice award), and reported over 50 active enterprises in its pipeline.

Panel reaction was largely positive but zeroed in on the 12% take rate on gross bookings as rich, prompting questions about who actually pays it; the response pointed to high-margin, hard-to-price-online items like private jets for VIP clients as the driver of margin above a typical OTA. Panelists also pressed on differentiation from a CRM with a connected booking engine and on defensibility against agent-based platforms using connectors, with the response emphasizing that fragmented trip costs (dinners, entertainment outside the travel program) are hard to capture elsewhere, that Swivul is itself agent-based with MCP support, and that its value is centralizing understanding of travel intent and ROI. A final question raised that not every trip delivers a measurable financial outcome, drawing audience laughter about the very conference they were attending, before the session closed with applause.

## Key Takeaways

### 💵 Swivul monetizes through blended travel margins

Swivul combines a company subscription with a 12% blended take rate on supplier margins and markups, with higher-margin services such as private jets and exclusive VIP access helping differentiate its economics from a traditional online travel agency.

Quotes:

> We have a blended take rate markup and commission, but we have things that you can't buy online, so we do things like private jets for the VIPs at Kraken... That's what gets our margin to go up significantly rather than a traditional OTA.

### 🤖 AI can recommend trips before employees ask

Rather than waiting for a traveler to request a destination, Swivul analyzes company context and proactively recommends where someone should go and why.

Quotes:

> Most platforms wait for me to say I need to be in Florida. Swivul says you should be in Florida and tells me why.

### ⚡ Move from travel intent to checkout faster

Swivul claims that its integrated workflow for bookings, approvals, policies, and receipts takes employees from intent to checkout five times faster than leading travel platforms.

Quotes:

> We can take companies and their employees from intent to checkout 5 times faster than leading travel platforms.

### 🔄 Improve policies and recommendations with every trip

Swivul uses accumulated travel data to help companies refine travel policies and make smarter recommendations over time.

Quotes:

> The system learns. We help companies by creating smarter policies and improving recommendations with every single trip.

### 🛒 Unify the full business trip in one workflow

Swivul brings flights, lodging, transportation, dining, events, and other business-critical experiences into one intelligent cart and checkout, helping companies measure the trip’s full cost rather than isolated travel expenses.

Quotes:

> Swivul brings all of that in into one intelligent cart, and that's how we can measure the entire cost of the trip, not just the flight and the hotel.

### 📈 Conferences can be evaluated as pipeline opportunities

The platform can identify the potential commercial value of an event, including pipeline and high-value meetings that were not previously on the user's radar.

Quotes:

> Sky found 3 million dollars worth of pipeline in the room and 15 high value meetings as well as this pitch.

### 💰 Cost does not equal business value

The cheapest option may not be the best investment when missed meetings, transportation, and lost opportunities are included in the calculation.

Quotes:

> A $700 hotel across the street from three critical meetings can create more value than a $400 hotel once you account for missed meetings, rides and the people you never got to meet.

### 🎯 Plan the entire trip around advancing a deal

When given a trip's participants and purpose, Sky can use CRM context to structure the experience around a specific opportunity and its stage in the sales process.

Quotes:

> Sky identifies a $10 million opportunity in my CRM currently in negotiation with the goal of moving to close one and then she builds the entire experience around closing it.

### 🧭 Travel decisions should start with business purpose

Swivul frames business travel as a question of whether a trip should happen and what it should accomplish—not simply how to book it.

Quotes:

> What you just watched looked like a story about booking a trip—it wasn't. It was about deciding whether that trip should happen in the first place.

### ✅ Post-trip workflows can close the measurement loop

After travel, Sky checks whether meetings occurred and whether related opportunities progressed, using CRM and calendar data without requiring additional work from the team.

Quotes:

> After the trip Sky closes the loop: did the meetings happen, did the opportunities move?
