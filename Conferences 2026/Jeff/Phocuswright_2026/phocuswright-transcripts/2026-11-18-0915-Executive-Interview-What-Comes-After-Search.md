## Summary

The session opened with a pitch for Lola, a new AI concierge built for experiences such as restaurants, concerts and sporting events, with travel as a secondary use case aimed at a local audience. It runs on a membership model akin to a credit-card perk program but without requiring a credit card, offering personalized recommendations and booking access, and had been live for only 30 days at the time of the conversation. Clips from past interviews were played showing earlier skepticism about starting a new travel company and about subscription models in travel, which were acknowledged with amusement, with the point made that travel is infrequent while going out locally is a much more frequent habit, making membership economics work better outside pure travel.

On operations, Lola was described as being built by a tiny 14-person team based at the Empire State Building, with a $100 million commitment from Booking Holdings directed not at overhead but at marketing to build trust and at the high inference and compute costs of running the product. Early success metrics being tracked include thumbs up or down product feedback, repeat usage, virality, and ultimately bookings, with an acknowledgment that no AI agent platform, Lola included, has yet cracked converting discovery into transactions, illustrated by an audience show of hands on using Muse or Instinct for travel bookings. Unlike horizontal agents relying on inefficient browser-based computer use, Lola was positioned as partnership-based, with direct API integrations with partners like GetYourGuide, Viator, Hertz, Clear, Blade and Duffel to ensure faster, more accurate and bookable experiences, a structure framed as a defensible differentiator against better-capitalized entrants such as OpenAI following its acquisition of Odesia.

Within Booking Holdings' broader portfolio, Lola was distinguished from the sibling initiative Veyo, with Lola focused on experiences and Veyo focused on owning the itinerary layer by ingesting bookings regardless of source and surfacing things like price-drop alerts; the two were described as friendly, non-competitive sister projects led by counterparts who talk regularly. The stated purpose of Lola within the group is less about rivaling legacy brands like Kayak or Orbitz and more about proving what a small, AI-first team can build quickly, including new features, new channels like WhatsApp or text, and a new tech stack that larger, legacy-constrained brands can later adopt. On why big incumbents struggle to move as fast, the discussion pointed to the complexity and real P&L risk of rebuilding legacy software and operations while still running the business, likened to rebuilding an airport while keeping it operational, versus Lola's low-risk experimentation with a small, pre-monetization user base.

Looking at the wider competitive landscape, it was argued that in an AI-agent-driven future, companies furthest from actual supply and content have the most to lose, while those controlling unique inventory, such as Marriott and Airbnb, are better positioned, with Booking Holdings seen as currently oversold on this perception. Small OTAs, ad networks and under-capitalized AI startups without strong brands were flagged as most at risk of being squeezed out. Among big tech players, Google was called the biggest travel company in the world for two decades despite not reporting it as a distinct vertical, and tipped to keep winning via products like Gemini; Meta's Muse was seen as a fun but likely money-losing product given inference costs vastly exceeding transaction value. A lean team building the biggest possible company was reiterated as the long-standing operating philosophy, extending from Kayak to Lola, with heavy use of automation such as AI-generated blog content and marketing replacing traditional hires.

Closing advice to entrepreneurs emphasized building a strong team, solving a genuinely worthwhile problem, raising ample capital as a hedge against being wrong, and forming durable partnerships given the industry's long memory. On what travel and hospitality brands need to do to stay visible and relevant, the key recommendation was to invest in loyalty strategy rather than continuing to pay for anonymous, one-time customer acquisition through channels like Google, redirecting that spend into direct-booking incentives following the playbook of credit card programs and brands like Marriott, while still allowing AI agents permissioned access and even charging them for it. A rapid-fire round closed the session, calling human travel agents irreplaceable, loyalty programs growing in importance, subscriptions niche but probably profitable, and travel startups too numerous, alongside quick, favorable remarks about industry figures Paul English and Glenn Fogel.

## Key Takeaways

### 📅 Frequent local outings make subscriptions more viable than travel alone

Lola focuses on activities people pursue regularly in their own city because travel happens only a few times per year and is less suitable for a monthly subscription.

Quotes:

> Travel is something most people do 3 or 4 times a year, right? So you can't really ask them to pay for it every month.

### 🏢 Incumbents face a high-risk modernization challenge

Established travel companies must modernize legacy software, teams and operating structures while continuing to run profitable businesses, making transformation slower and riskier than it is for a small startup like Lola.

Quotes:

> Imagine the complexity of rebuilding an airport at the same time you have to operate the airport. And that's what all these big brands have to do. And it's tremendously complex. If they make a mistake, it has real P&L implications.

### 🧪 A lean AI team can serve as an innovation test bed

With only 14 people, Lola is designed to demonstrate what a small, AI-first operation can build quickly, test new business models and features, and automate for the wider Booking Holdings group.

Quotes:

> My role within the group is to prove what's possible. Right? To demonstrate what you can do with a small team, what features you can build very quickly. ... We have a new tech stack, a new AI-first harness.

### ⚠️ AI may squeeze intermediaries without unique supply

As AI becomes a primary interface, small OTAs, ad networks and AI companies may be vulnerable if they lack distinctive inventory, sufficient capital or a trusted brand; companies closer to unique supply may be better positioned.

Quotes:

> I think the companies that have the most to lose are the ones that are furthest away from the content, from the gears. ... That's all these small OTAs, all these small ad networks, it's all these small AI companies who have built a product but still haven't raised enough capital to build a brand.

### 🛒 AI agents must turn discovery into transactions

A central challenge for Lola and other AI agents is changing consumer behavior so users complete bookings through the agent instead of switching to established booking platforms or contacting providers directly.

Quotes:

> Anyone who's in the AI agent space, whether you're a horizontal or vertical, is, how do you convert from being discovery and recommendations into a transaction?

### 🧭 Build for pivots and protect partnerships

Hafner’s startup advice is to build a strong team, solve a problem worth solving, raise enough capital to create room for pivots, and protect industry relationships because partnership networks and reputations endure.

Quotes:

> Build a great team, solve a problem worth solving, and go raise as much capital as you can, because you're probably going to be wrong about both your team and the problem you're going to solve. But with capital, you have the oxygen to pivot if you need to. And then the last part would just be: form partnerships. Don't burn the people you're doing partnerships with.

### 🔌 Direct APIs are Lola’s execution advantage

Lola is using direct, partnership-based API integrations with companies including GetYourGuide, Viator, Hertz, CLEAR and Blade to deliver faster, more accurate content and bookings than computer-use browsing.

Quotes:

> We wanted direct connections on an API basis. So that you have rapid responses. They're accurate. The content's broad. You can actually book through it. And it's really efficient.

### ❤️ Loyalty will matter more after search

Travel and hospitality companies should shift some customer-acquisition spending from anonymous, transaction-by-transaction search toward loyalty benefits that encourage customers to book directly and give the brand permission to serve them.

Quotes:

> This whole idea of acquiring consumers anonymously one transaction at a time through third parties like Google, it's going to be dated in a few years. ... Redirect some of that in terms of benefits to your members, to book directly with you.

### 🤖 Lola applies AI concierge technology to experiences

Lola is positioned as an AI concierge for restaurants, concerts, sporting events and travel, with its initial focus on helping local audiences discover and access things to do.

Quotes:

> It's all about being an AI concierge for experiences. So restaurants, concerts, sporting events, yes, we do travel as well, but it's really built for, like, local audience.

### 💰 AI products require funding beyond product development

For AI agents, scaling requires investment not only in product development but also in marketing that builds consumer trust and in the high inference and compute costs of delivering the experience.

Quotes:

> The $100 million commitment from Booking isn't spent on overhead. It's being spent on marketing awareness to build trust, and it's being spent on inference or compute cost because doing what we do is actually really expensive.

### 🧪 Lola is still testing for product-market fit

After its first 30 days, Lola is monitoring user experience feedback, repeat usage, sharing and bookings to determine whether the product is gaining traction.

Quotes:

> We're only 30 days into it, so we're looking for product-market fit right now. We're seeing when people are using the product, do they give us a thumbs up or thumbs down on the product experience signal? We're watching closely repeat rates. We're looking for virality. Are people sharing the product? And then the last thing that we're looking for is bookings.

### ⚙️ Lola targets the effort involved in going out

The central problem Lola seeks to solve is the friction of deciding what to do and arranging it, by combining personalized suggestions with access to the selected experience.

Quotes:

> We solved the problem of people want to go out and they want to go out with the least amount of effort required.
