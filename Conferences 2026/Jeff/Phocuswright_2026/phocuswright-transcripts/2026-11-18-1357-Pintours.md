## Summary

The session opened with a pitch for an airline retail personalization platform that runs inside travel brands' own websites and booking flows, letting partners keep the customer relationship and user behavior data instead of losing it to third-party redirects. The presenter sized the opportunity using the roughly 2.6 billion annual air passengers, a conservative 2% capture rate, and an average $500 transaction value to arrive at a $26 billion market opportunity, and described current traction as 92 named accounts across 22 countries spanning 35 airlines/airports, 42 hotel brands, and 9 government-run destination boards, translating into a $261 million revenue opportunity.

Audience voting on this pitch was uniformly positive, but judges pressed on the plausibility of the 2% attach rate, drawing on the historical difficulty of cross-selling travel ancillaries (the old Expedia analogy of airline tickets as 'milk at the back of the store'). The presenter defended the number by explaining capture happens through two channels—embedding on the brand's website and inside the airline's native booking flow—using data the airline already shares. Further questions covered how hotel and activity content is sourced (partnerships with OTAs, bed banks, and other aggregators for hotels, tours, rental cars, and airport transfers) and how personalization works despite limited first-time user data, relying on airline/hotel-provided demographics, loyalty tier, mileage history, and past bookings, supplemented by simple onboarding questions about trip preferences.

The next pitch introduced an AI 'expert layer' for travel experiences, framed as a way to scale VIP tour-guide expertise that currently costs thousands of dollars per private tour and can't reach most guests. While general AI tools can already plan and book trips, the founder argued they still rely on static content like maps and audio tours and lack the deep, human expert knowledge that distinguishes a true VIP experience; the company's patented interview process is positioned to extract that expertise and embed it on top of leading LLMs. Proof points included powering experiences at Alcatraz and the Empire State Building, and delivering fan experiences across host cities during the World Cup through a partner. The product was shown applied across verticals—tours and activities via an AR demo, cruises as an end-to-end concierge and excursion planner, rail and aviation journeys replacing static maps with personalized AI guidance, and hospitality concierge services—monetized through revenue share or licensing, with the company already supplying an estimated 5–10% of experiences offered by major OTAs.

Audience reaction was mostly positive with one skeptic, and judges raised harder questions in the Q&A. One concern invoked the 'bitter lesson'—that raw computing power tends to overwhelm curated human interfaces over time—questioning whether the human-expertise advantage would hold against fast-improving general AI. Others asked why the company focuses on B2B rather than direct-to-consumer, to which the answer was a desire to power the biggest brands given the scale of opportunity in B2B, and whether it competes with or replaces players like Viator or GetYourGuide; the founder clarified it instead powers those OTAs, supplying a meaningful share of their experience inventory. Pressed on why not simply specialize in being the expert layer for curated venue experiences, the response reaffirmed that this is already the core focus, with interviews revealing tour guides' decades of undocumented knowledge as the company's key differentiator. On scalability, the founder described a patented onboarding process combining existing property materials (maps, PDFs) with a two-to-three-hour expert interview to stand up a new experience in a matter of hours.

Pintors AI says it captures VIP-level knowledge from expert interviews through a patented process, then embeds that expertise on top of leading large language models. > We take that expertise and embed it, enabling the next generation of experiences... Models are commodity, expertise isn't.

### ⚠️ The 2% attach-rate assumption was challenged

### 🧳 Travel brands can keep the customer and transaction in-house

The company embeds personalization and booking across the entire trip inside travel brands, avoiding redirects to third-party platforms. This is intended to preserve the partner’s customer relationship, behavioral data, and customer intelligence.

Quotes:

> We personalize the entire trip. And we allow the users to book everything within the travel brand. And the partner keeps the customer.

### ⚙️ A patented process aims to scale expert onboarding

Pintours says it can convert an establishment’s maps, PDFs, and other onboarding materials, together with a two- or three-hour expert interview, into an AI experience layer within a few hours. This is its answer to the scalability challenge of curating expertise for individual venues.

Quotes:

> We developed a patented process that can do this in a couple hours... We can do that with a 2 or 3 hour interview, and that's all good to go.

### 💰 Pintors monetizes through licensing and bookings

Quotes:

> We do revenue share for experiences booked or we do a licensing model where brands can embed it into their systems and just pay us the license fee.

Quotes:
