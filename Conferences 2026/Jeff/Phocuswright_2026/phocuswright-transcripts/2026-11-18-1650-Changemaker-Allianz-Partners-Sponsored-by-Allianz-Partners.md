## Summary

The session opened as an informal bar-side conversation between representatives of Allianz Partners, the global leader in travel insurance running the US and Canadian business, and Boundless Labs, a company operating primarily in the cruise and complex-travel space that is building a fully functioning online cruise booking platform designed to make complicated travel products feel bookable with confidence.

Discussion turned to how to reduce friction in selling something as complex as travel insurance inside a B2B2C booking flow, where consumers are visiting partner sites to book core travel, not insurance. Allianz described its Core Fusion technology, trained on two billion quotes a year, which consumes itinerary and consumer data to surface a relevant, simplified insurance offer rather than overwhelming travelers with choice—for example, not pushing comprehensive cancellation coverage on a refundable air ticket, or highlighting electronic device protection for travelers with kids. The goal is to support conversion rather than slow it down, since partners need shoppers to move quickly into the booking cart.

On the cruise side, the conversation highlighted how uncommoditized and complex cruise products are—differing ship by ship, with varied deposit schedules and countless add-on decisions around dining, excursions, and entertainment—and how travel technology has largely failed to address this complexity. Boundless Labs noted that this gap created the opportunity to build its platform, which now sees 91% of bookings completed without any human touch, and that the company has recently pivoted toward a B2B model to bring this frictionless experience to established brands building their own e-commerce journeys.

The panel then explored how AI agents are shifting from search and comparison tools toward recommendation and transactional roles, and what that means for an industry built around human-consumable booking paths. Allianz raised the risk that agents trained primarily on speed and price might never surface travel insurance at all, illustrated by an anecdote about a personal agent who had never proactively mentioned travel insurance until prompted, and who offered to stop mentioning it altogether. At the same time, there was seen to be real opportunity: unlike humans, AI agents can digest large volumes of coverage details, exclusions, and comparisons instantly, potentially enabling an assisted-sale model where quality and reliable delivery on promises become the deciding factor as more choice becomes feasible to present.

On the cruise-specific AI outlook, it was noted that full AI booking agents for cruise remain further off than in other travel verticals given the product's complexity, but AI is already proving valuable for discovery and cross-sell—feeding content to AI tools that shape opinions before consumers reach the site, and using AI-informed testing and merchandising to grow joint travel-protection attach rates by 70% year over year, which also eases customer service when cruise disruptions occur, such as ships departing without delayed passengers. Closing remarks affirmed that AI tools benefit both human and AI agents, with human agents backed by AI assistance since no person can recall the details of 30,000 individual sailings, underscoring a symbiotic relationship between the two companies' technologies.

## Key Takeaways

### 🤖 Agentic commerce creates training risks and new opportunities

As AI evolves from search and comparison toward recommendation and transactions, agents may omit travel insurance if they are trained mainly for speed and price. But they can also instantly process coverage, exclusions and comparisons, creating opportunities for assisted recommendations and making product quality more important.

Quotes:

> The risk is that agents that have been trained maybe in speed and price maybe will never offer travel insurance. The opportunity is that agents can digest all of the coverages, exclusions and comparison shop kind of instantly.

### 🎯 Relevance can prevent unnecessary coverage

Insurance recommendations should reflect what the traveler has already purchased; for example, a refundable air ticket may make comprehensive trip-cancellation coverage unnecessary.

Quotes:

> If you’re buying a refundable air ticket, you don’t need a comprehensive trip-cancellation product.

### 📱 Simplified protection must fit the core booking journey

Travelers visit partner websites and apps to book trips, not to shop for insurance. Protection therefore needs to be presented in a small amount of digital space, with complex coverage simplified enough to support rather than disrupt the primary conversion.

Quotes:

> People are going to those websites and mobile apps not to buy travel insurance. They’re there for core booking. Our challenge is to take something as complex as travel insurance—we have dozens of covered reasons—and really simplify it.

### 🧠 AI agents may need a different booking experience

Travel companies built around human UI and UX may need separate booking paths for AI agents, with information structured for machine decision-making rather than only for human browsing.

Quotes:

> You may have two separate booking paths. One for an agent. One for a human. You’ve got to be able to give data to the agent.

### 📈 AI can boost protection attach rates and service outcomes

AI-assisted discovery and cross-sell experimentation are helping Allianz and Boundless grow travel-protection attachment, with the speakers reporting 70% year-over-year growth in attach rate. Protection also makes customer service easier when cruise disruptions occur.

Quotes:

> Through those tests and through really helping feed those models, our attach rate on our joint products has grown 70% year over year. If something goes wrong with the cruise and some of these products, it makes our customer services job a lot easier.

### 🚢 Cruise booking is high-value and highly complex

Cruises are not commoditized products: travelers compare different ships and manage deposits, dining, excursions, entertainment and other add-ons, often making a high-ticket purchase with substantial decision complexity.

Quotes:

> Cruise tends to be one of the highest ticket items in travel today and probably the most complex. Cruises are not commoditized.

### 🧑‍💻 AI can augment human cruise agents

AI tools can help human agents manage the scale and complexity of cruise inventory, including thousands of ships and sailings, rather than replacing human assistance altogether.

Quotes:

> We do provide them with AI tools as well because a human cannot remember the details of a single ship, let alone 30,000 sailings that we have on our platform.

### 🤝 Complex-travel technology is moving into B2B partnerships

Boundless Labs has pivoted toward partnering with established brands so they can incorporate a more seamless cruise-booking experience into their own e-commerce journeys.

Quotes:

> We recently pivoted pretty strongly to the B2B space so that we can partner with established brands to bring that experience to them.

### 🤖 Core Fusion personalizes insurance offers

Allianz’s Core Fusion technology uses itinerary data, consumer data and learned preferences to recommend a travel-protection product that is more relevant to the customer and booking segment.

Quotes:

> It consumes itinerary data, consumer data, learns about preferences and then really is giving an offer for a product that the consumer will value.

### ⚡ Frictionless booking can automate most cruise sales

Boundless Labs reports that 91% of its bookings do not require human assistance, demonstrating the potential for a seamless digital flow even in complex travel categories.

Quotes:

> 91% of our bookings do not touch a human.
