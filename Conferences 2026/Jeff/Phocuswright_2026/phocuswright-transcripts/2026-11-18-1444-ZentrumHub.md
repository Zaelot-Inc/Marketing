## Summary

The presentation opened by describing a structural problem in travel distribution: sellers are wired into dozens of wholesalers and DMCs, so a single property search in a market like Dubai can trigger more than 500 supplier calls. Despite rates often returning within two seconds, 90% of the time sellers still end up waiting on the slowest suppliers, meaning more supply sources translate into slower answers rather than better rates.

This inefficiency is set to worsen as AI enters the distribution chain: look-to-book ratios have already climbed from historic levels of 100-to-1 or 1,000-to-1 to roughly 300,000-to-1 because of meta platforms, caching and proliferating distributors, and experts expect a further tenfold increase as bed banks and agencies deploy their own AI agents and MCP servers. The talk framed this as a shared cost problem: travel sellers pay to query suppliers and suppliers pay to answer, yet 60% of the suppliers queried for the cheapest rate only win 5–10% of overall bookings, meaning everyone pays but nobody really knows where to ask.

Positioning itself as the entity that sits between sellers and suppliers and processed over 1 million bookings last year, the company announced the launch of ZentrumIQ, an AI layer for the distribution ecosystem with two products: Verdict for travel sellers, which identifies the properties most likely to sell and the suppliers most likely to be competitive so sellers get the best rate at the best speed regardless of how many suppliers they're wired to, and Pulse for suppliers, a first-of-its-kind analytics tool showing where a supplier is winning or losing business and why—illustrated by a supplier strong in Southeast Asia that keeps paying for Miami searches it can never win competitively. Pilot results on Verdict showed a 30% performance improvement and a 45%+ reduction in supplier calls without sacrificing best pricing.

Looking ahead to an agentic era where agencies run their own MCP servers and agents, the pitch argued that someone needs to know what to ask and whom to ask, positioning ZentrumIQ—already integrated into the company's booking engine API—as that layer, with MCP integration planned next since agents will only be as smart as the data feeds they can access. The company reported pilot customers including 10+ sellers on Verdict and two suppliers on Pulse, operating across a market representing €267 billion in sales and 400,000 distributors, summarizing ZentrumIQ as the rails for optimized distribution built for best rate, best speed, and the coming agent-driven landscape.

Audience and panel reaction was mixed. One panelist disclosed that WebBeds, which works with the company, was pleased with the product. Another pressed on differentiation in a crowded hotel API space, prompting a response that the edge comes from global coverage and cross-channel search data (meta platforms, TMCs, B2Bs) that lets the model learn which suppliers perform best for specific hotels rather than just regions. A more skeptical panelist argued the approach addresses the wrong root cause, contending that the real fix is reducing unnecessary searches by surfacing accurate pricing and identifying unlikely-to-win supply at the source, and suggested predictive AI as a better alternative to layering optimization on top of what was called an existing “spaghetti mess.” The presenter acknowledged partial agreement but maintained that sellers currently lack a way to know which supplier to query when, which is the gap the product aims to close, conceding this was only an initial step.

## Key Takeaways

### 💸 Both sides pay for inefficient searching

Travel sellers incur costs while requesting answers, and suppliers incur costs responding to queries—often from markets where they have little chance of winning the booking.

Quotes:

> Travel sellers are paying to ask for answers while suppliers are paying to answer those queries.

### 🌐 Fragmented supply creates massive search complexity

Travel agencies source rates from wholesalers, DMCs and other agencies. A simple Dubai property search can trigger more than 500 supplier calls, illustrating how fragmented distribution has become.

Quotes:

> A simple search for properties in Dubai translates to more than 500 calls across suppliers.

### ⚡ Verdict prioritizes likely winners before querying suppliers

Verdict is designed to identify which properties are most likely to sell and which suppliers are likely to be competitive, allowing the platform to optimize the search rather than query every connected source equally.

Quotes:

> It automatically identifies which are the properties which are most likely to sell and which are the suppliers that are actually going to be competitive.

### 🤖 ZentrumIQ targets both sides of travel distribution

ZentrumHub launched ZentrumIQ as an AI layer for distribution, with Verdict for travel sellers and Pulse for suppliers. It is already integrated into the booking-engine API, with MCP support identified as a next step for the agentic era.

Quotes:

> Today we are launching ZentrumIQ, the AI for the distribution layer that sees both sides... It is already integrated into our booking engine API, and MCP is something next we are looking at.

### 📊 Verdict pilot reports fewer calls and better performance

In its pilot, ZentrumHub reports a 30% performance improvement and a more than 45% reduction in supplier calls, while maintaining the best price.

Quotes:

> We already see a performance improvement of 30% and reduction in call of over 45% across suppliers, all this without losing on the best price.

### 🎯 Suppliers can reduce spending on unlikely opportunities

Pulse is intended to expose mismatches between a supplier’s strengths and the searches it receives. For example, a supplier with strong Southeast Asia inventory may learn that it is spending on Miami searches where it is unlikely to be competitive.

Quotes:

> It is really good in Southeast Asia... and it is still getting the searches for Miami where most likely it is never going to be competitive.

### 🧭 Pulse shows suppliers where they win and lose

Pulse gives suppliers market- and region-level analytics showing where their distribution performs well, where it loses, and why.

Quotes:

> Pulse lets suppliers know how it is performing in different markets, different regions, where it is winning, where it is losing and why it is losing.

### 🧪 Panelists challenge the layer being optimized

Panelists questioned whether optimizing which suppliers receive searches addresses the root cause of distribution inefficiency. They suggested improving pricing at the source and using predictive AI to determine likely outcomes before adding another optimization layer.

Quotes:

> I think by the way you're attacking the problem the wrong way... if you actually go to the root cause and figure out the actual pricing before at the source itself... you will automatically reduce searches.

### ⏱️ More supply can make rate responses slower

Connecting more suppliers does not automatically improve the customer experience: a search may be delayed by its slowest supplier even when most rates arrive within seconds.

Quotes:

> 90% of the time, even though you are getting your rates within 2 seconds, you still end up waiting for your slowest suppliers.

### 📈 AI is driving look-to-book ratios sharply higher

The distribution ecosystem was built for human search behavior, but AI agents and MCP servers are expected to generate many more queries. The look-to-book ratio has reportedly risen from roughly 100–1,000 searches per booking to 300,000–1.

Quotes:

> Not too long ago, we were happy with our look-to-book of 100 to 1, 1,000 to 1. With meta platforms, caching and so many distributors, the look-to-book has already gone to 300,000 to 1.
