## Summary

The discussion opened around community presence, framing the franchise model as a deliberate way to claim and serve a local area, with Vacation Planners of Salt Lake City cited as an example of a travel agency rooted in its community. This was offered as a response to concerns that people and local businesses have become disconnected from each other.

On technology, the position was clearly not anti-tech: there was no interest in cutting out technology companies, and AI was described as an opportunity for advisors rather than a threat. The conversation framed the real challenge as balance — figuring out where technology helps versus where human interaction remains essential, noting that the industry's core job is simply to get people to leave their homes and have a valuable vacation experience that also satisfies suppliers.

A central proposal was for the industry to stop segmenting travelers into rigid categories — OTA customers versus those who want human advisors — and instead work together. The idea was that companies already holding strong consumer relationships, whether or not they are in the travel business, such as People Magazine or Southern Living, represent untapped opportunities for collaboration with travel advisors and technology platforms alike.

This connects to the importance of advisors being physically present and engaged within their local communities, serving the simple desire of people wanting to go on vacation. It was noted that despite unprecedented access to technology, demand for the human touch has not diminished, and that the advisory role itself is expected to grow substantially — with a figure cited from prior panels suggesting it could quadruple in the coming years.

The session closed on a forward-looking vision: bringing the worlds of technology and human advisory together to reach consumers who have the propensity to travel but have been stalled somewhere in the process, whether by over-reliance on technology without human follow-up or by a human interaction that failed to close the deal. The closing message was that whichever company figures out how to unify these approaches first will come out ahead.

## Key Takeaways

### 🧩 Match the right travelers with the right suppliers

A successful travel ecosystem should create value not only for the traveler but also by helping suppliers reach customers who are a good fit for their offerings.

Quotes:

> We want to make sure the suppliers are being fulfilled with the right customer.

### 📣 Trusted relationships outside travel can unlock new demand

Companies outside traditional travel may already have trusted relationships with people likely to travel. The opportunity is to connect those audiences with travel expertise instead of leaving the relationship fragmented.

Quotes:

> The company that gets this, the company that understands they already have a relationship with the next traveler, whether they're in the travel business or not.

### 🏘️ Local franchises can restore travel businesses in communities

A franchise model is presented as a way to establish recognizable local travel businesses and bring the travel relationship back into the community.

Quotes:

> Our franchise model is designed to claim an area for that reason. Vacation Planners of Salt Lake City. Oh, that's a community. That's my local travel agency.

### 🎯 Partnerships can recover travelers who did not book

A major collaboration opportunity is identifying people with a propensity to travel who did not purchase because a technology-only journey or a human interaction failed to close the sale.

Quotes:

> We start tapping in to consumers that we know are wanting to, that would have a propensity to go on vacation, but they've done something that has prevented them from buying.

### 🧑‍💼 Human advice is becoming more valuable alongside technology

Despite unprecedented access to technology, the speakers see growing demand for human interaction and expect the travel advisory role to expand significantly.

Quotes:

> We have more access to tech than we've ever had before, yet we seek the human touch.

### 🌍 The industry’s job is to deliver valuable vacations

The speakers define the industry’s core job as helping people leave home, take worthwhile vacations, and have experiences that work for both travelers and suppliers.

Quotes:

> We need humans to leave their homes, go on vacation. We need to make sure that that vacation was a great value for them.

### 🔄 Technology and human guidance should work as one journey

The session rejects an either-or choice between technology and people: AI and other tools can help advisors and consumers when digital convenience is combined with human guidance.

Quotes:

> You're not against technology, but, you know, it's kind of balancing, you know, where does technology help us? Surely AI is just an opportunity for the advisor.

### 📍 Advisors need an active community presence

Travel advisors are encouraged to engage directly where customers live, serving people locally while remaining connected to broader technology ecosystems.

Quotes:

> You need to be there as an advisor. You need to be in the community. We want to be in the community. We want to serve the community.

### 📚 Media and lifestyle brands can extend travel distribution

Brands that already engage audiences around lifestyle and culture, such as magazines, could collaborate with travel companies to connect their relationships with vacation planning.

Quotes:

### 🤝 Travel businesses should collaborate across models

OTAs, technology companies, suppliers, and travel advisors should work together instead of treating their customer segments and channels as separate worlds.
