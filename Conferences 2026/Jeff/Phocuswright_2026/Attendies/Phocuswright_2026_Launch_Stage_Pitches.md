## Format
- "Launch, presented by Spotnana." Each company gets 6 minutes to show a new product and answer "why now," then a focus group questions them and rates how likely the product is to succeed. The audience votes with smiley or frowny cards and a people's choice vote in the app.
- Stage history (Phocuswright): 682 companies since 2008; 527 raised $14.7B; 150 acquired.
- **Focus group (judges):** Chelsea Salamone (Principal, Thayer Investment Partners), Ellen Keszler (CEO, Clear Sky Associates), Erik Blachford (Founder, Pine5 Ventures), Kristina Carter (VP Global Partnerships, Perk), Kurien Jacob (Partner, Highgate Technology Ventures), Michael Berk (Director of Strategic Innovation and Partnerships, Amex GBT), Roger Sharp (Chairman, WebBeds Group and North Ridge Partners), Steve Singh (Executive Chairman and CEO, Spotnana; Madrona), Traci Mercer (CCO, Priceline). Moderator: Pete Comeau (MD, Phocuswright).

## Judge questions, by company

### 1. Fareportal: Travel OS
- What funding do you need, and what is the first priority? (Answer: Partner OS, so partners go live fast.)
- What makes this innovative versus a souped-up affiliate program?
- How are you different from the other companies building the same thing?

### 2. Letsfly: Postra
- Who is the customer, and what is the business model? Is this meant for third parties or for Letsfly itself?
- Who is your ideal customer? Which OTA, for example? Big OTAs often build this in-house.
- Won't this problem get solved at the infrastructure layer instead of by a separate servicing product?
- Why focus on OTAs and not TMCs? TMCs get airline content through the GDS.
- Have you launched with a real partner outside Letsfly? (Answer: one live partner in Europe.)

### 3. Globaleur
- Is a 2% attach rate realistic? Cross-selling travel has always been hard ("airline tickets are the milk at the back of the store").
- Where do hotel and activity content come from? (Answer: OTAs, bed banks, aggregators.)
- How do you understand the traveler beyond their stated intent? What personalization are you actually bringing, with little first-time data?

### 4. Pintours
- Does the "bitter lesson" apply? Won't raw AI compute beat curated human expertise over time?
- Why B2B instead of direct to consumer?
- Do you compete with or replace Viator and GetYourGuide? (Answer: they power them.)
- Why not just focus on being the expert layer for curated venues for a while?
- How hard is it to scale? (Answer: about 2 to 3 hours of expert interview per new experience.)

### 5. Voyani
- What differentiates you from the ten other companies building this?
- How do you know what the in-trip spend is? Are you tied to a credit card?
- What about spend outside your network?
- You don't see card providers as a competitor?
- If you must be the merchant, won't pieces of the trip be missing?
- What attach rate or booking rate have you seen since launch? (Answer: beta with 2 partners.)

### 6. Dfinitiv
- Airlines and card companies already run loyalty. How does this add to that, and how does it integrate with their programs?
- Why would all these loyalty programs connect to you, without scale?
- If I'm a platinum member, how do I know it actually got me the better seat?

### 7. Gadget Korea
- Can a visitor on a postpaid phone plan (not your eSIM) still use the app? (A judge noted a destination marketing organization is interested beyond Korea.)
- Lots of eSIM providers exist and could do the same. What stops them? It came across as just another eSIM company, not a super app.
- How do you get distribution outside Korea?

### 8. AltexSoft: Interlace
- Does the ROI come from token savings or labor savings?
- Isn't software engineering itself becoming obsolete? Is this just for companies without good prompt engineering?
- How is the value measured? Is there a mismatch between the companies that need this and the ones excited about it?

### 9. ZentrumHub: ZentrumIQ
- How are you different in a crowded hotel API space? (A WebBeds judge said WebBeds is happy with the product.)
- Aren't you fixing the wrong root cause? Shouldn't we cut unnecessary searches at the source with accurate pricing and predictive AI, instead of optimizing on top of the "spaghetti mess"?

### 10. Groupize
- Feedback more than questions: the problem is real and the approach is strong.
- Can incumbents with big corporate customer bases build the same AI quickly?
- This may be a tough market from an investor's point of view.

### 11. TRIPCREATOR AI: Marco Polo
- Are you really building two products, a developer model and a crowded app layer?
- Does it book trips? (Answer: no, booking stays in the operator's system.)
- What stops the general LLMs from building their own large travel model? (Answer: "there is no moat"; the edge is speed and distribution.)
- Is the model distilled from existing LLMs? (Answer: no, built from scratch on their own data.)

### 12. Swivul
- 12% of gross bookings seems rich. Who is paying it? (Answer: high-margin items you can't buy online, like private jets for VIPs.)
- How is this different from a CRM with a booking engine connected that AI could run?
- Why not use connectors (e.g., a CRM cloud) for one-way links? As things move to agents, how do you defend Swivul? (Answer: Swivul is agent-based, supports MCP, and owns the view of travel intent and ROI.)
- Roger Sharp: not every trip has a financial outcome. (The room laughed: how many people at Phocuswright are delivering one?)

## What the judges kept asking (patterns)
1. **Who is the customer, really?** Letsfly, Voyani, TRIPCREATOR and Globaleur were all pushed to name one buyer.
2. **What's the moat when general AI keeps improving?** Pintours (bitter lesson), TRIPCREATOR (LLMs catching up), Swivul (agents with connectors), AltexSoft.
3. **Can the incumbent just do this?** Groupize (Cvent and big platforms), Gadget Korea (other eSIM providers), Letsfly (big OTAs in-house), ZentrumHub (fix it at the source).
4. **Is the number real?** Globaleur's 2% attach rate, Swivul's 12% take rate, AltexSoft's ROI.
5. **Steve Singh's wrap-up (Building the Next Generation of Travel, 3:20 PM):** too many startups try to solve too many problems; pick one and own it. He said three of the companies were ones he would "love to write a check behind" (he did not name them). The 95% of the trip after booking (servicing) is where cost and dissatisfaction are; United shutting off agentic agents shows that selling alone is not enough.

## Jeff's pick: Swivul
- **What it is:** a travel and entertainment operating system for companies. The AI concierge, Sky, connects CRM, calendar and goals to outside events, recommends which trips are worth taking, books the whole trip (flights, hotels, dinners, events, private jets) in one checkout, and then checks the CRM to show which trips moved deals.
- **People:** Sarah Figueroa, Co-Founder and CEO (presented). Luciano Bracco, Co-Founder and CTO, is in the Phocuswright app (V3 CSV).
- **Proof points they gave:** Mercury partnership (300,000+ business customers); 50+ enterprises in pipeline; won people's choice at the BTN conference; "agent-based" with MCP support.
- **Why it fits Zaelot (hypothesis):** early-stage, AI-native, integration-heavy (CRM, calendars, GDS, dozens of APIs, MCP). A startup like this may need extra engineers to keep up with enterprise demand. Not in HubSpot (not checked yet).
