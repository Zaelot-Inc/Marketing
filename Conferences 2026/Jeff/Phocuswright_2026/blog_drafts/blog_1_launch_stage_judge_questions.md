## What the judges asked 12 travel startups at Phocuswright

I sat through the Innovation Launch stage at Phocuswright on Wednesday. Twelve companies. Six minutes each. Then a panel of investors and travel executives asked hard questions.

The pitches were good. The questions were better. Here is what I heard.

### The companies

- **Fareportal Travel OS:** a white-label travel portal for brands like banks and loyalty programs.
- **Letsfly (Postra):** AI plus experts to handle flight changes, refunds and add-ons for OTAs.
- **Globaleur:** an AI agent that plans and sells the whole trip inside an airline or hotel's own site.
- **Pintours:** turns expert tour-guide knowledge into an AI layer for attractions, cruises and rail.
- **Voyani:** an in-trip app that holds your bookings and sells tours, eSIMs and local transport.
- **Dfinitiv:** brings airline and hotel loyalty status, miles and cards into ChatGPT and other AI assistants.
- **Gadget Korea:** a travel super app for visitors to Korea, built on an eSIM.
- **AltexSoft (Interlace):** maps a company's code so engineers and AI coding tools know what a change will break.
- **ZentrumHub (ZentrumIQ):** tells hotel sellers which suppliers to ask, and tells suppliers where they win or lose.
- **Groupize:** AI agents that run corporate meetings and group travel end to end.
- **TRIPCREATOR AI:** a travel-specific model that builds bookable itineraries from a tour operator's own catalog.
- **Swivul:** an AI concierge for company travel that tells you which trips are worth taking, books them, and measures the return.

### The questions came down to four

**1. Who is the customer?**
This came up more than anything. Letsfly was asked three different ways which OTA it wants to sign. Voyani was asked how it is different from "the ten other people building this." TRIPCREATOR was asked whether it is building one product or two.

**2. What's the moat when AI keeps getting better?**
Pintours was asked about the "bitter lesson," the idea that raw computing power beats hand-built expertise over time. TRIPCREATOR was asked what stops the big AI models from building their own travel model. The founder answered honestly: "there is no moat." The edge is speed and distribution.

**3. Can the incumbent just do this?**
Groupize was asked whether big platforms with thousands of corporate customers can build the same AI fast. Gadget Korea was asked why other eSIM companies couldn't copy it. ZentrumHub was told it might be fixing the wrong problem.

**4. Is the number real?**
Globaleur claimed a 2% attach rate on hotels and activities. A judge reminded the room that cross-selling travel has always been hard. Swivul's 12% take rate got the same push: "Who's paying the 12%?"

### My favorite: Swivul

Swivul starts with a question most travel tools skip: should this trip happen at all?

Its AI looks at your CRM and calendar, finds the trips worth taking, books everything in one checkout, and then checks whether the meetings happened and the deals moved. It treats travel like an investment, not a cost.

The best moment came from Roger Sharp. He pointed out that not every trip has a financial outcome. The room laughed, because we were all sitting at a conference.

### What I took away

Every company on that stage was using AI. The judges didn't care about that. They asked who buys it, why now, and what happens when the big players show up.

That's a good test for any product. It's a good test for services too.

What question would you have asked?
