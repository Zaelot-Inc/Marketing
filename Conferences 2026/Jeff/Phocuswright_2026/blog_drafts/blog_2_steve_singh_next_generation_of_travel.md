## Steve Singh: stop building for the 5% of the trip

Steve Singh co-founded Concur. Today he runs Spotnana, which sponsored the Innovation Launch stage at Phocuswright. Right after watching a dozen startups pitch, he sat down on Center Stage and said what he thought.

Here are the five points that stuck with me.

### 1. Pick one problem

His feedback on the pitches was simple: "Pick one problem, nail that problem."

He said too many startups were trying to solve too much at once. He did say there were three companies on stage he would "love to write a check behind." He didn't name them. He wants them tighter.

### 2. The trip is 95%, the booking is 5%

Most travel innovation goes into how we sell. Very little goes into the rest of the trip.

In his words, most problems are not in "the 5% of the time to actually buy something; it's in the 95% of the time from the moment you leave your front door through the trip and all the way back."

He used United's recent decision to shut off AI booking agents as an example. Agents that only sell push cost onto service teams. They have to do more than book.

### 3. There are two customers

The first is the traveler, the person actually taking the trip.

The second is the travel seller. He said that customer "has not been well served."

Everyone else in the middle has to be clear about the value they add.

### 4. AI-native means rethinking the work

Adding AI to an old product is not AI-native. He asked how a process would work "in a world where you don't have to think about the legacy workflows."

He compared it to how Concur reinvented expense reports instead of digitizing the paper version.

### 5. Trust is the business

He wants a richer picture of each traveler, beyond travel history. But only if it's used to make the trip better. "That trust is everything."

He also said the old corporate travel software model is changing. Card companies like Ramp and Brex subsidize the software to win card spend. "The equation that Concur operated with is no longer valid," he said, coming from the person who built Concur.

### What I took away

Steve's message matched what the judges asked all afternoon. Know your customer. Solve one problem. Build for the whole trip, not just the purchase.

For those of us who build travel technology, the biggest opening is in the 95%: servicing, changes, the moments after the booking where travelers get stuck.
