## What went right, and it is worth copying

This is the only event in the series that was run with a plan on both ends, and it shows in the numbers.

The recap describes a June 24 check-in a week before the event that covered partnership status, an outreach plan to Vercel AEs via Crossbeam, and LinkedIn content to run around the event. Then two scheduled follow-ups after, on July 22 and July 29.

Compare that to the pattern in the rest of the series: show up, capture some contacts days or weeks later, never work them. Here there was a pre-brief, a target list, a content plan, and booked follow-ups. The result is three contacts instead of thirty, but one is an SQL and one is an actively worked `opportunity` two months later.

Small, planned, and followed through beat large and unworked at every other event this year.

## What this says about event strategy

On process, this is the model event of 2026. Pre-event check-in, a named outreach plan, content run around it, and two booked follow-ups. Three contacts, one SQL, one live `opportunity` still being worked two months later, and a joint workshop in motion.

On measurement, it is incomplete in the same way as everything else: no deal records, no briefs, and a September commitment that exists only in prose. The relationship is real and moving, and none of its value is quantified anywhere.

The lesson for 2027 is not to attend more events. It is that this event's preparation pattern, a week-ahead brief and pre-booked follow-ups, converted at a rate nothing else came close to. That pattern costs almost nothing to apply to every other event on the calendar.
