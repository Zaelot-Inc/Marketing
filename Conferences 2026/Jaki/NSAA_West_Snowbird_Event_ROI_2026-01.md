## What this says about event strategy

On the measures available, this trip produced little, and what it may have produced is unclaimed. On the narrow reading, three contacts from a 690-person room, none past Lead, no briefs, no deals, no revenue, and a thirteen-day lag before anything reached the CRM. On the wider NSAA-sweep reading, add Brian Demarest, an SQL with 20 notes on a real telemetry-and-data conversation, and Neill Pieper at OutdoorProlink.

The two outcomes the 2026 recap credits to this show, Inntopia and Alterra intelligence, both trace elsewhere on inspection. But the recap undersells something real: the SNOWsat thread is the most developed relationship the ski circuit has produced, and it is not mentioned in the Snowbird section at all.

That is not an argument that NSAA is the wrong show. The 2025 NSAA import is genuinely the largest ski-industry dataset we own, Alterra came out of that ecosystem, and the SNOWsat SQL came out of the circuit. It is an argument that **we attend these shows without a capture plan and without a follow-through plan.** There was no target list built before Snowbird, no meetings booked in advance, no intake mechanism to turn a conversation into a record, and when the imported list finally got used it got one undifferentiated blast.

The useful question for 2027 is not whether to return to NSAA. It is whether we return with that apparatus in place: a named target list going in, meetings booked ahead of the floor, and every contact entered within 48 hours of the show closing. Without it, a trade show produces relationships that exist only in one person's head, and this report is what that looks like on paper.

This trip still has no cost figure. Until it does, the return cannot be quantified either way, and that remains the highest-value thing anyone can add to this report.

The honest summary: the ski circuit has given us one genuine SQL, one large but contaminated contact database, and an Alterra relationship that closed. None of those three is being actively worked right now. That is a follow-through problem, not a venue problem.
